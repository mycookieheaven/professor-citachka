## Honesty, accuracy and scope boundaries honoured in the content

- **Education, not advice.** No lesson recommends a security, fund, account, allocation, contribution amount, insurance product, container, lender, pricing decision or tax position for an identifiable person. Lesson 61 closes its application with "not a recommendation about which obligation to hold"; 62 does not endorse either offer and ends by noting the exercise says nothing about affordability; 63's rubric is arithmetic on stated assumptions; 66 states that break-even is a threshold rather than a forecast; 68 ends with "not tax advice for your business"; 70 states that a plan ending in a product recommendation has stopped being a memo and that the lesson is education, not individualized financial or investment advice.
- **Factor rate is not an APR.** Stated explicitly in lesson 62 and demonstrated numerically: one factor rate of 1.30 corresponds to about 51.44%, 67.15%, 96.64% and 109.29% annualised depending only on the payment schedule, and the naive 60% simple annualisation is identified as an error. 12 CFR 1026.22 is quoted for what an APR is required to capture, with the note that its tolerances are disclosure-accuracy rules rather than price limits and that it is written for consumer credit.
- **Net proceeds differ from gross.** Lesson 63's entire mechanism, with the 25,000/23,750 and 100,000/97,000 cases, and the grossed-up-principal case where a factor applied to a fee-inflated amount implies about 135% on the money received.
- **Timing and compounding stated every time.** Each worked example names the periodic rate, the payment timing, the compounding convention and the horizon, usually with an explicit exclusion list. Lesson 62's whole point is that the same total cost implies different annual rates under different schedules; lesson 57's nominal-against-real distinction is reused and referenced rather than restated.
- **Tax and regulatory facts distinguished from interpretation.** The IRS trust-fund page, the retention periods and the New York registration rule are cited as statements of the cited source, with jurisdiction and period named. Interpretation is separated: whether a particular worker is an employee or a contractor is flagged in lesson 68 as a question of fact and law that the cited sources do not decide; whether commercial financing must disclose an APR is flagged in lessons 62 and as a jurisdiction-specific question; the retention periods are labelled as US federal income and employment tax rules that states, other regulators and contracts may extend.
- **Rules vary by jurisdiction and change over time.** Stated in lessons 62, 63, 66, 68 and 69, and in the reading notes for the CDTFA and New York sales-tax sources, which are presented as examples of one jurisdiction's framework rather than as universal rules. IRS Publications 15 and 538 are identified as annual publications to be read in their current edition.
- **No guaranteed returns, no false affiliations, no pressure tactics.** No projection is presented as a promise, every rate is labelled as an assumption, lesson 70 requires a falsification condition and names the crossover rate at which the decision reverses, and no lesson contains urgency language or an appeal to a brand relationship.
- **Source recency disclosed.** The SBA Office of Advocacy brief is dated August 2017 and is labelled as a historical publication whose product-structure description is the reason it is cited. The Federal Reserve small-business survey is a March 2026 report of self-reported firm experience and is labelled as describing a population, not a business. The two CFPB tool PDFs are labelled November 2018 and the toolkit page June 2020. The CDTFA publication is the January 2026 edition. OpenStax sections carry the publisher's own copyright date on the page.

## Unresolved limitations

- **Parent verification still required.** Rendered layout, the text-sequence visuals as displayed, reading links as clicked, assessment wiring, accessible narration and production behaviour were not tested here by design: no server, no browser automation, no deployment.
- **Word counts sit above the two most recent finance continuations.** 17,391 words for ten lessons (1,557-1,911 each) against 15,905 for the published 51-60 level, 12,460 for 41-50 and 10,440 for 31-40. The extra length carries the rate-basis conversion set in 62, the bridge and runway work in 64, the allocation and mix cases in 66, the price-and-volume split in 67, the pay-period decomposition in 68 and the two-criteria decision in 70. If the parent enforces a house style nearer 1,100 words, the compressible fields are `depth.application` and `depth.summary`; no worked arithmetic needs to be removed.
- **Two source-hygiene findings need to travel with the pack.** The stale `what-is-amortization-en-1941` slug and the archived CFPB blog page are both live HTTP 200 responses that serve the wrong or empty content. Any future automated link checker will mark them green. Both are excluded from the pack, and the live replacements were read.
- **No blocked URL is disclosed, because none was hit.** All 33 cited URLs returned 200 to a browser user agent in this environment. If the parent's pipeline sees 403 on `consumerfinance.gov`, `cdtfa.ca.gov` or `fedsmallbusiness.org`, that is a change in behaviour rather than something this pack observed. `dfs.ny.gov` did return 403 and is not cited.
- **Some sources are JS-rendered.** The OpenStax sections, several CFPB Ask pages and the IRS retention page load part of their text through the browser. Their content was confirmed through the web-extraction tool, raw-HTML marker searches or PDF text extraction, and where a reading note directs the reader to an accordion or an expandable section, that structure was observed. The one place where a page's body could not be read directly is resolved by not relying on it: no claim rests on text that was not inspected.
- **No independent subject-matter review.** The content has not been reviewed by a licensed adviser, a tax professional, an accountant or a lender. The amortization, break-even, ratio and memo models are arithmetic under stated assumptions; the payroll and sales-tax figures use illustrative rates on a stated jurisdiction; the retention periods are US federal rules.
- **Every model is a model.** Constant rates, level payments, constant margins and mixes, no fees unless stated, no taxes unless stated, no behaviour change during the horizon except where quantified, and no capacity constraint unless stated.
- **The quizzes remain binary retrieval checks.** They are not evidence of professional competence, and the lessons do not present them as such. The unit and level questions are longer than the lesson prompts and are intended as application checks, not certification.
- **Question ID scheme reconciled with the uniqueness rule.** The brief's `uq-61-70-a` to `-e` "per unit" was implemented as `-a` to `-e` for unit 1 and `-f` to `-j` for unit 2 so that IDs stay unique, which `attachCoursePacks` enforces and which matches the published 61-70 packs in five other subjects. If the parent intended literal duplication, that intent is incompatible with the validator and should be resolved at integration rather than by weakening the check.
