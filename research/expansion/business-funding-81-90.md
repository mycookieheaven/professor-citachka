## Funding-honesty compliance

- **A factor is never described as an interest rate or an APR.** Every commercial cost is stated as a dollar amount per $100 of net proceeds, a total repayment amount, a payment amount and frequency, or a date. Lesson 90's contrast case says explicitly that no annual percentage rate is computed. The one nominal interest rate in the pack (lesson 86) is attached to an illustrative term loan and is presented as a note rate with a monthly payment and a total of payments.
- **Gross advance and net proceeds are separated** in 81, 85, 86, 88 and 90; the $57,264.96 figure in lesson 90 is explicitly net proceeds, and lesson 85 states $34,500 of repayment on $25,000 of net as $138 per $100 of net.
- **Fees, remittance, reconciliation, guarantees, UCC filings, stacking and jurisdiction** are all addressed directly: SBA upfront versus annual service fees that a lender may not charge to the borrower, plus prepayment penalties on long maturities (84, 86); remittance expressed in weeks and dollars throughout; reconciliation defined operationally in 85 and 89; guarantees in 87 with joint-and-several exposure, limited guarantees and the signature rule; UCC perfection, five-year effectiveness, the six-month continuation window, authorization to file, termination on authenticated demand and the information statement in 82, 84, 85 and 89; stacking in 85 with the FTC staff formulation and its true costs; Virginia and Utah disclosure and registration law stated within their own scope with no claim of nationwide application (85, 89).
- **No approval, funding, rate, relief or outcome is promised anywhere**, and no lesson contains a guarantee of approval. Declining is presented as a legitimate, often useful conclusion with reasons and conditions. Nothing suggests deception, coercion, concealment or manufactured urgency: lesson 82 explicitly refuses to describe a permanent disqualifier as fixable so that a sale stays alive; lesson 86 refuses to relabel an ineligible use; lesson 88's rubric instructs the learner to scan their own draft for pressure phrases; lesson 87 requires the guarantee to be explained in writing before signature.
- **Commission conflicts are disclosed honestly** in two places. Lesson 81 states that on a $60,000 advance at three percent the advisor would earn $1,800, which is 92.07 percent of the merchant's $1,955 loss on the failed path, and calls the asymmetry the reason the screen must be written down. Lesson 90 requires the advisor's own position — what we are paid, on what contingency, and what we cannot promise — to be explicit in the decision.

## Unresolved limitations (for the parent)

1. **Structural replication, not the parent's runner.** The `attachCoursePacks` invariants plus the five-lessons/five-questions expectation were reimplemented in Python and pass, but no TypeScript validator or vitest suite was executed. `search_files` found no `scripts/validate-packs.ts` in the repository, so the parent should confirm which validator it intends to run and run it after registration.
2. **The pack is in `staged/` and is therefore not loaded.** `course-pack-registry.ts` imports explicit paths and never glob-loads, so nothing is published until the parent moves the file into `src/lib/course-packs/` and imports it after `businessFunding7180`. The registry comment requires teaching order, which this pack satisfies.
3. **Two readings describe a script-rendered page rather than a plain-text one.** The eCFR section views for 13 CFR 120.150 and 120.110 resolve (HTTP 200) but render their section text by script, so the wording was read from the GovInfo CFR XML editions and each note discloses that. A reader in a browser sees the same sections; an automated checker scraping raw HTML will see navigation only.
4. **Two candidate hosts are bot-blocked to command-line fetches and were excluded, not cited with caveats.** `sec.gov` and `dfpi.ca.gov` both returned 403. Since neither host survived into the pack, no reading note carries a 403 disclosure — but a parent-side link checker that treats 403 as broken will still flag those hosts if they are added later.
5. **Legal and compliance questions are flagged, not resolved.** Loan-versus-purchase characterisation, guarantee enforceability, filing priority in a specific state, the reach of a state collection regime, and whether the federal consumer collection statute touches a particular obligation all require qualified review. The lessons say so and refer the merchant to a lawyer; a reviewer may want the caveats strengthened.
6. **Marketplace and enforcement material is dated.** The FTC staff perspective is February 2020; the deception statement is 1983; the unfairness statement is likewise historical; the RAM Capital press release is January 2022 and describes allegations and a stipulated order with other defendants' cases ongoing. None of it is presented as current market practice or as a finding about any firm.
7. **No real client data and no practitioner review.** All merchants, amounts, terms, dates and dialogues are fictional. No practising underwriter, broker or lawyer reviewed the content, and passing these assessments is explicitly not professional certification.
8. **Not delivered in this batch:** registration, renderer or application tests, audit-script updates, production build, deployment, HTTP readback, and any visual or audio verification — all parent-owned. No claim is made that this pack is live.
