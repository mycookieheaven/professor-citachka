Question 1

A millwork shop is approved for $150,000 at a 1.34 factor. The settlement sheet shows a 3.0 percent origination fee, a 2.0 percent broker fee, a $38,400 payoff of the prior position, $212.05 of prior per diem, a $95 filing fee and a 5.0 percent escrow held for thirty days. The owner asks when the $150,000 will arrive. What do you tell him, and what is the cost per $100 of the money he can actually use?

Question 2

An auto glass merchant on a $205,920.00 obligation with forty weekly debits of $5,148.00 has the week 11 debit returned R01 and the week 12 debit returned R01 again. The contract adds a $50 return fee and a 5 percent late charge and treats two returned debits in ninety days as an event of default. What may be re-presented, what is added to the balance, and what does the catch-up debit look like?

Question 3

A print shop owes $46,200.00 on a $85,800.00 obligation and receives a payoff quote of $46,402.62 dated 3 November, good through 10 November, accruing $18.42 a day. The wire leaves on 12 November. The funder's servicer says the file is closed. What is wrong with that sentence and what does the merchant still owe?

Question 4

A pharmacy's $78,000.00 a month of card settlements is assigned, and a 45 percent participant sends the merchant a notification instructing it to pay $35,100.00 of each month directly to the participant and $42,900.00 to the servicer. The merchant keeps paying the servicer in full because that is where it has always paid. Who is discharged, and what is the exposure after three months?

Question 5

A logistics company has paid 26 of 40 weekly debits of $4,140.00 on a $165,600.00 obligation and is offered a renewal: $60,000.00 of new money, a 3 percent origination fee taken out of the new money, and the $57,960.00 balance rolled in at a 1.36 factor over 52 weeks. The broker presents it as a 1.36 deal that lowers the weekly payment. What is the price of the new money and what is the weekly payment?
