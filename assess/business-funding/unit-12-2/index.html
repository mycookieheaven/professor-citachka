Question 1

Ninety days of statements show $1,284,600.00 of deposits, $1,190,200.00 of debits and an average daily balance of $22,410.00. A first-position remittance of $5,148.00 a week is proposed, equal to 70.89 percent of monthly net cash. The merchant is profitable and has never bounced a supplier cheque. Why does the first debit still risk an R01, and what measurement shows it?

Question 2

A merchant wants to contest a $9,400.00 overcharge. The contract accelerates the balance when the funder deems itself insecure and requires arbitration before an arbitrator 1,900 miles away, with the merchant advancing half the arbitrator's compensation, estimated at $12,500.00, plus $18,000.00 of counsel and $1,450.00 of travel. What is the clause doing, and how should it be described?

Question 3

A file shows revenue rising from $412,000.00 to $1,401,000.00 in one year, a bank account opened 41 days before the application, three statement PDFs with identical creation timestamps and a tax return whose receipts are exactly $116,400.00 above the deposits in the statements. The merchant says the statements are 'just missing a couple of accounts'. What do you do, and what do you not say?

Question 4

A merchant complains that a $3,300.00 debit was taken eleven days after the file was paid off. Your twelve-month log shows three complaints across 214 funded files, one about that debit, one about a disclosed fee and one about a call after an opt-out. What is the response and what does the log tell you?

Question 5

At day ninety on a $205,920.00 file funded on 7 November, thirteen weekly debits of $5,148.00 were expected. The bank shows fifteen debit entries totalling $77,220.00 and two credits of $5,148.00, plus $140.00 of bank fees and two $50.00 funder return fees that were debited separately. The servicer's ledger shows the balance reduced by $66,924.00. Reconcile it.
