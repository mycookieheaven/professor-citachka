Unit quiz: More than one line: voice leading, non-chord tones, countermelody, harmonic rhythm and texture

Check understanding, separately from reading

5 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This quiz checks the lessons in this unit. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Saving a lesson records reading only. Previous lesson completions have not been converted into passing scores. You can continue reading without a pass; the assessment remains unpassed.

Question 1

A four-part setting of G, C, D7, G at 72 beats per minute doubles the leading tone F sharp in the soprano and tenor, and moves soprano and alto from D and G to E and A, a perfect fifth apart, in similar motion. What are the two faults, what is a corrected soprano and alto pair, and how long is one 4/4 bar?

Question 2

Over a bar of C major the melody holds G from the previous bar into the downbeat and falls to F on beat 2. Later, on a weak beat, it leaps a fourth up to A and returns at once by step to G. A learner labels both notes passing tones. What are they, and what evidence decides?

Question 3

Against an eight-note cantus firmus in G major at 60 beats per minute, a learner writes a second line that starts a tenth below and moves in parallel tenths for six notes before leaping to the final G. Using first-species rules, what is wrong, and how long does the exercise last?

Question 4

An eight-bar phrase in C major at 100 beats per minute moves one chord per bar: C, A minor, F, G, C, A minor, D7, G7. A producer who wants a bigger arrival doubles the chord rate in bars 5 to 8. What happens to the durations, and what would broaden the arrival instead?

Question 5

The same eight-bar progression is played three ways: a solo guitar melody with no accompaniment; guitar chords strummed in the same rhythm as the vocal; guitar chords plus an independent bass line while the vocal phrases across the bar lines. Name the three textures and say what changes between the second and third.
