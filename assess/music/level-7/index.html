Cumulative level test: Playing with other people and capturing it honestly: charts, count-ins, first improvisation, and three takes you can defend

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Saving a lesson records reading only. Previous lesson completions have not been converted into passing scores. You can continue reading without a pass; the assessment remains unpassed.

Question 1

A quartet reads a 32-bar AABA chart at 90 beats per minute. They agree on a four-click count-in, and one player starts playing on the fourth click rather than after it. Computed from those figures, how long is one performance of the form, how long is the count-in, and what is the starting player's error in seconds?

Question 2

A lead sheet contains an eight-bar A section with a repeat sign, then an eight-bar B section, at 96 beats per minute. A player counts the phrases to plan entries. How many bars are performed, how many four-bar phrases does that give, and how long is the whole form?

Question 3

Two players rehearse a duet. Each uses headphones that carry mostly their own instrument, both are accurate alone, and together the tempo drifts by about one and a half seconds over the form. Which change is best supported by the ensemble-timing evidence and by the recording practice in this level?

Question 4

A learner practises a difficult passage for forty minutes and makes ten complete runs, two of which contain an error. The session rule was nine correct runs out of ten before raising the tempo. Using the practice evidence and the capstone's requirement of stated criteria, what is the correct next action and why?

Question 5

A soloist improvises over a sixteen-bar Am7 to D7 vamp using A minor pentatonic and fills every bar with eighths. A listener asks for more shape. Combining the improvisation and phrasing material in this level, which revision addresses the request?

Question 6

A learner records a keyboard part by capturing the instrument's audio output, then wants to correct one wrong note without replaying the part. Which statement about the options is accurate, and what should the learner change next time?

Question 7

A learner decides to quantise an audio guitar part by slicing twelve late notes out of the take and moving each onto the grid. Each slice creates two joins, and each join is covered by a fade of about 10 ms. Computed from those numbers, how much fade time does the edit introduce in total, and what is the larger musical risk?

Question 8

A chart handed out at a rehearsal shows a single block of 32 bars with no section labels. Using the conventions for sections and phrases in this level, what is the most useful first observation to raise with the group?

Question 9

Two rhythm players rehearse a four-bar vamp at 100 beats per minute and both strum on every beat, which sounds loud and vague. A listener suggests giving them different jobs. Using the texture and ensemble material in this level, which assignment is best, and what is the resulting spacing between their attacks?

Question 10

A learner reports practising 60 minutes without a break, monitoring through headphones loud enough to hear detail, and hearing ringing afterwards, then asks how to make the next session more productive. Which response is consistent with this level's practice and safety guidance?

Question 11

A player adds the flat fifth to A minor pentatonic to make the blues scale over an Am7 to D7 vamp, then holds that note for a whole bar as the D7 arrives. Using the blues-scale material and the chord-tone reasoning in this level, what is the accurate description of what happened?

Question 12

A learner completes the capstone: three complete takes of a 76.8 s part, one chosen, a written reason naming the timing criterion, and a render of the chosen take as a 16-bit stereo WAV at 44.1 kHz. Which package statement is accurate about the deliverable?
