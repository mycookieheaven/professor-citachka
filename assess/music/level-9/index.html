← Music course pathLevel 9 · Cumulative level test

Cumulative level test: Many lines and real players: four-voice leading, non-chord tones, a countermelody, transposition, ranges, spacing and tempo that breathes, ending in a scored three-part arrangement

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Saving a lesson records reading only. Previous lesson completions have not been converted into passing scores. You can continue reading without a pass; the assessment remains unpassed.

Question 1

In a four-part phrase in F major at 84 beats per minute, the chordal seventh of C7, which is B flat, is doubled in alto and tenor and both voices then leap down to A. What is wrong, what should happen, and how long is an eight-bar phrase?

Question 2

A melody over a bar of G7 sounds C on the downbeat, holds it while the chord changes, and then falls to B. In a second bar of G7 the same C is reached by a leap from G, accented, and resolves to B. Are these the same non-chord tone, and how do you tell?

Question 3

A learner writing a countermelody against a cantus firmus in D Dorian reaches the final unison by having both lines rise a fourth into it. Using first-species rules, what is wrong and what should the last two notes be?

Question 4

A twelve-bar blues at 120 beats per minute uses one chord per bar with a quick change in bar 2, then a second version holds the tonic for the first four bars. What are the durations, and what does the change do to the phrase?

Question 5

A producer stacks the same four-chord loop three ways: a single synth line; the synth plus block chords on every beat; the synth, block chords and a bass playing a separate riff. How does the texture change, and what should be checked in the arrangement?

Question 6

A horn in F is given a concert-pitch part in A major running from A3 to F sharp4. What written key and range should the horn player see, and how many sharps does that written key carry?

Question 7

A tenor is asked to sustain A4 for twelve bars at 66 beats per minute in a four-part chorale texture. Using chorale-based ranges, what is the problem, how long is the passage, and what is the best fix?

Question 8

An arranger doubles the third of a C major triad in three octaves across a string section while leaving the root once in the bass. What is the likely result, and how should the doubling be redistributed?

Question 9

A pianist records a take without a click and then wants it on the grid at 88 beats per minute. What can be done to the MIDI part and what can be done to the audio, and why are the two different?

Question 10

You must deliver a sixteen-bar piece at 92 beats per minute for B flat trumpet, cello and piano, with a written ritardando in the last bar to 69. What has to be checked for each player, and what is the total duration?

Question 11

A melody uses an accented appoggiatura on the downbeat of every second bar while the harmony changes twice per bar at 104 beats per minute. What is the effect, and how long does each chord last?

Question 12

A four-part ending in E flat major has the bass on E flat2 and the tenor a minor third above on G flat2, with soprano and alto a third apart an octave higher. Using spacing and voice-leading rules, what is the fault and the correction?
