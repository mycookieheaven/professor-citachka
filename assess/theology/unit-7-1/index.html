Question 1

Two students argue about the phrase in Dei Verbum 11 that Scripture teaches without error 'that truth which God wanted put into sacred writings for the sake of salvation'. One says the Church therefore restricts inerrancy to faith and morals and leaves the rest of Scripture free to be false; the other says the Church did not restrict it that way. Which reading is correct, and why?

Question 2

A homilist tells the congregation that 'Rome has quietly abandoned the historical character of the Gospels; modern Catholic exegesis now shows they are the community's creation, and the old teaching no longer binds'. His assistant replies that the Pontifical Biblical Commission requires the historical-critical method and Dei Verbum 19 still stands. Who is right, and what is the precise position of the Church?

Question 3

A catechist claims that the Church 'changed her teaching' when the doctrine of the Church grew from the apostolic faith to the definitions of Nicaea and Chalcedon, and therefore any later doctrine can be reversed. A second catechist replies that genuine development never contradicts what came before. Which account matches the Church's own texts?

Question 4

At Chalcedon the definition was accepted by the imperial church and not by the Coptic, Syriac, Armenian and Ethiopian churches, which remain separated from it today. A student asks what that does to the authority of a council's definitions. What is the accurate answer?

Question 5

A parish bulletin states: 'Since Vatican II taught that the Church rejects nothing true and holy in other religions, all religions are paths to salvation, and interreligious dialogue replaces evangelisation.' Which correction is accurate?
