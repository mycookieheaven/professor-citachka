Unit quiz: Cases of Change and Non-Change: Capital Punishment, Slavery, Indulgences, Sacramental Validity and Liturgical Discipline

Question 1

During a parliamentary debate on abolishing the death penalty, one Catholic legislator says the Catechism's word 'inadmissible' is only a policy preference that legislators may disregard, while another says the 1992 edition of the Catechism, which allowed recourse to the penalty in principle, taught something the Church has now admitted was wrong. Using the 2018 letter of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith and Evangelium Vitae 56, assess both.

Question 2

A teacher tells a class that because the Church tolerated slavery for centuries and now teaches that it is intrinsically evil, doctrine is simply the reflection of social consensus; a student replies that the Church always condemned slavery in principle, so nothing changed at all. Using Gaudium et Spes 27, Veritatis Splendor 80 and the 1839 letter In Supremo Apostolatus, give the accurate account.

Question 3

A parish bulletin announces: 'Plenary indulgence for the faithful departed: enrol your deceased relatives for a donation of twenty pounds; all temporal punishment and the guilt of sin are remitted.' A parishioner asks whether this is what the Church teaches. Using the Council of Trent's decree on indulgences, the Catechism 1471-1479 and Paul VI's Indulgentiarum Doctrina, identify each error in the announcement.

Question 4

A diocese discovers that for fifteen years baptisms in one parish were conferred with the formula 'We baptise you in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit'. Using the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith's 2020 response and the canons of the Council of Trent on the sacraments, state what has happened, what must now be done, and why the minister's good intention did not save the rite.

Question 5

Two Catholics argue about the 2021 motu proprio Traditionis Custodes. The first says it abrogated the traditional Latin Mass, proving that the 1962 Missal had been doctrinally defective all along. The second says no pope can restrict the older rite, because the Mass of Pius V was established by the Church's immutable tradition and a pope has no authority over it. Using Sacrosanctum Concilium 22, Summorum Pontificum article 1 and Traditionis Custodes article 1, adjudicate.
