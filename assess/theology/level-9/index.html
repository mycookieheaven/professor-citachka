Cumulative level test: Doctrine About Doctrine: the Church as an Object of Faith, the Grades of Assent, and the Cases Where Teaching Develops, Is Reformed, or Holds Fast

Check understanding, separately from reading

Question 1

A study day asks participants to sort four claims into 'defined', 'authoritative but not defined' and 'legitimate theological opinion'. The claims are: (1) the one Church of Christ subsists in the Catholic Church; (2) a definition of the Roman Pontiff is irreformable of itself and not by the consent of the Church; (3) the 1962 Missal was never juridically abrogated; (4) the precise theological note by which the reservation of ordination to men is taught. Which sorting is correct, and why?

Question 2

A Catholic magistrate in a state that retains capital punishment must sentence a convicted murderer. She accepts the Catechism's statement that the death penalty is inadmissible but argues that in this case the prisons are unsafe and the convict has already killed a guard. Using Catechism 2265-2267 and the 2018 letter of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, what may she conclude, and what may she not conclude?

Question 3

A parish receives an adult who was baptised as an infant by a Methodist minister, with water and the Trinitarian formula, and who now wishes to be received into full communion. A volunteer says she must be baptised conditionally 'to be safe', and another says she must be baptised absolutely because Protestant baptisms are not real baptisms. What is the correct pastoral and doctrinal judgment?

Question 4

An ecumenical commission issues a report saying that the disagreements between East and West at Florence in 1439 were 'merely cultural misunderstandings about purgatory and papal authority', and that since the union was never received the doctrinal questions remain open. Using the Florentine decree, Lumen Gentium 25 and Pastor Aeternus, assess the report.

Question 5

A seminarian preparing a presentation lists four papal acts: the definition of the Immaculate Conception in Ineffabilis Deus (1854); the definition of the Assumption in Munificentissimus Deus (1950); Ordinatio Sacerdotalis (1994); and an apostolic exhortation on the family. He asks which are infallible, and on what basis, using the four conditions of Pastor Aeternus and the grades of Ad Tuendam Fidem.

Question 6

A school history textbook states that 'the Church sold forgiveness of sins in the late Middle Ages, until Luther protested and Trent quietly abolished the practice'. Using the Council of Trent's decree on indulgences, Paul VI's Indulgentiarum Doctrina and the Catechism 1471-1479, correct the sentence.

Question 7

A textbook asserts that 'the Catholic Church never condemned slavery until the 1960s, and only changed its mind when public opinion made the old position untenable, which shows that its moral teaching is simply the morality of the surrounding society'. Using papal documents from 1537 and 1839, Gaudium et Spes 27, Veritatis Splendor 80 and the 1866 reply of the Holy Office, evaluate the assertion.

Question 8

A national conference of bishops issues a unanimous statement on a contested public question and describes it as 'the teaching of the Church in this country'. A journalist says it is therefore infallible and a Catholic politician who disagrees is a heretic. Another commentator says a bishops' conference has no teaching authority at all. Using Lumen Gentium 25, Christus Dominus and Ad Tuendam Fidem, assess both.

Question 9

A priest announces that he will celebrate Mass according to the 1962 Missal in his parish church every Sunday, citing Summorum Pontificum's statement that the older missal was 'never abrogated', and adding that no bishop can restrict a rite that the Church has used for centuries. Using Traditionis Custodes articles 2, 3, 4 and 8 and the 2021 responses, state the canonical position and identify the theological error in his argument.

Question 10

A theologian publishes an article arguing that a particular teaching, which the Church has proposed as definitive, in fact belongs to the third paragraph of the Profession of Faith and should therefore be open to public dissent. A colleague replies that the classification is settled and the article is simply dissent. Which position is defensible, and what does each of them get wrong?

Question 11

A diocese declines to implement a Roman instruction on liturgical practice, and a commentator describes this as 'a clear case of non-reception, which in the Church's tradition means the instruction has no force'. Using the lesson on reception, Lumen Gentium 25 and the distinction between doctrine and discipline, evaluate the claim and say what a bishop's refusal does and does not establish.

Question 12

A candidate for ordination is asked in examination: 'State what is defined, what is authoritative but not defined, and what is legitimate opinion about the Church, and give one example of each.' His answer begins: 'Defined: that the pope is infallible whenever he teaches; authoritative: that the Church is the Mystical Body of Christ; opinion: whether non-Catholics can be saved.' Assess the answer.
