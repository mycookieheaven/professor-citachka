Cumulative level test: Dogma, Grace, and the Moral Life: Defining, Reading, and Living the Faith

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Saving a lesson records reading only. Previous lesson completions have not been converted into passing scores. You can continue reading without a pass; the assessment remains unpassed.

Question 1

A news report says a pope “declared” a contested policy position to be Catholic doctrine, and the report's verb is quoted onward by three commentators. The underlying act turns out to be a letter explaining a revision to a paragraph of the Catechism. Describe the correction a careful reader should make to the report, and describe what the letter itself claims.

Question 2

A homilist reads from a Catholic Study Bible and builds a doctrinal claim on a sentence that appears in the editorial footnote beneath the passage rather than in the sacred text itself. A listener, reading along, cannot find the sentence in a different edition. Explain what has gone wrong and what the doctrinal claim would need instead.

Question 3

A community introduces a changed practice and defends it on the ground that “the Church's doctrine develops, so our practice is apostolic,” while also treating a rule about the celebration as though it were irreversible doctrine. Identify both errors and state what the defence actually requires.

Question 4

A pamphlet states that the title Coredemptrix is now dogma because a pope used the word in a homily and a petition has been signed by many bishops. A reader asks what would be required for the claim to be true, and what status the title actually has.

Question 5

A discussion group concludes from the doctrine of justification by grace that confessing sins is a leftover of a pre-Reformation mentality, and that a person who repents privately need never approach the sacrament. Which sources answer the conclusion, and what do they say?

Question 6

A catechist explains that Baptism imprints an indelible character and concludes that a baptised person's later conduct cannot affect their standing before God, adding that Paul's moral commands in Romans 6 are directed only to the unbaptised. Assess both claims.

Question 7

A student defends ignoring the encyclical's teaching on intrinsically evil acts by noting that well-known Catholic theologians have criticised it, and concludes that a disputed teaching imposes no obligation. What distinctions resolve the case?

Question 8

A speaker says the canonisation of a saint adds a new article to the deposit of faith and proves that revelation continues in the Church today. A listener asks how canonisation should be classified and what this implies about the deposit.

Question 9

A leaflet defends soliciting money before a devotional image by citing the Council of Trent's decree on the veneration of images. Explain what the decree actually teaches and how a corrected leaflet would read.

Question 10

A hospital chaplain faces three requests on one afternoon: a conditional Baptism for a patient whose earlier Baptism used an unclear formula, the anointing of a patient with a chronic but stable illness, and a request to give general absolution to a large group without individual confession. Classify each and state what kind of reasoning answers it.

Question 11

A devotional article states that Mary dispenses graces at her own discretion and that the saints, being closer to God than we are, answer prayers by their own power. Rewrite the two claims within the Church's teaching using the sources of this level.

Question 12

You are asked to write a one-page briefing on a disputed ecclesial document that mixes a doctrinal claim, an argument that the claim is continuous with earlier teaching, a pastoral application, and a moral judgment about conduct. Set out the sequence of moves a competent briefing would make and the order in which it would make them.
