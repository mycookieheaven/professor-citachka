Question 1

In a parish discussion of Lumen Gentium 8, one participant says that because the Church of Christ 'subsists in' the Catholic Church, the Catholic Church is simply one branch among several, and another says that because the same paragraph mentions 'elements of sanctification and of truth' outside her visible structure, nothing at all is being claimed for the Catholic Church. Using Lumen Gentium 8 and the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith's 2007 responses, state what the sentence claims and what it does not.

Question 2

A diocesan newspaper describes a papal homily at a Wednesday audience as 'an infallible pronouncement', and a blogger replies that papal infallibility covers everything the pope says about faith, morals, politics and economics because Pastor Aeternus says his definitions are irreformable 'of themselves'. Using the text of the 1870 definition, identify the conditions it attaches and say whether either writer has read it correctly.

Question 3

After a Synod of Bishops issues its final document, a journalist writes that 'the synod has voted to change Church teaching and the pope must now implement it', while a canonist replies that the synod's vote was only consultative and therefore carries no weight at all. Using Episcopalis Communio and canon 343, adjudicate between them.

Question 4

A traditionalist website argues that because the Byzantine Church repudiated the union concluded at Florence in 1439, the Florentine decree on purgatory and on the Roman primacy was never 'received' and therefore never became binding teaching; a progressive website draws the opposite conclusion from the same history, that binding teaching is whatever the faithful end up accepting. Assess both arguments using Lumen Gentium 25 and Pastor Aeternus.

Question 5

Two students argue about John Paul II's 1994 letter Ordinatio Sacerdotalis. The first says it cannot be infallible teaching because the pope did not speak ex cathedra and the letter is not a definition. The second says it is a dogma of divine faith in the strictest sense, on the same footing as the definition of 1854. Using the 1995 response of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith and Ad Tuendam Fidem, state what is settled and what is a matter of theological classification.
