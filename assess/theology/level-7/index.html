Cumulative level test: Scripture Scholarship, Doctrine in Development, and Accountability: What the Church Defines, Permits, and Leaves Open

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Saving a lesson records reading only. Previous lesson completions have not been converted into passing scores. You can continue reading without a pass; the assessment remains unpassed.

Question 1

A study group reads Joshua's account of the sun standing still and a member concludes that since Scripture cannot err, the Church must hold that the earth stopped rotating; another member concludes that since the passage is poetry, nothing in Scripture can be trusted as historical. Which account of the Church's position is correct?

Question 2

A student asks whether the 2018 revision of Catechism 2267, which teaches that the death penalty is inadmissible, proves that any doctrine may change. What is the accurate answer?

Question 3

A parish discussion asks why Vatican II's teaching on collegiality and religious freedom binds, given that the council defined no new dogmas and that several hundred bishops voted against the final texts. Which answer is accurate?

Question 4

A Catholic and a Protestant friend compare notes: the Catholic holds that the Church's approval of a devotion, or a Pope's definition of a doctrine, can oblige belief, while the Protestant holds that nothing outside Scripture can oblige a Christian's conscience. Which statement of the Catholic position is accurate, and how should the Protestant objection itself be presented?

Question 5

Two priests discuss penance. The first says that because God's mercy is unconditional, any general absolution at a large gathering is justified; the second says that individual integral confession is the ordinary way, and general absolution only in the two cases the Church names. Which is correct, and what are those cases?

Question 6

A family keeps vigil at the bedside of a dying parent. They ask the priest whether the anointing will cure her, whether it is the last sacrament she may receive, and whether they should still pray for her after death. Which set of answers matches the Church's teaching?

Question 7

A parishioner is preparing to explain indulgences to a class and asks you, in one session, to state what the Church has defined about purgatory, what the practice of praying for the dead rests on, and what is not defined. Which outline is accurate?

Question 8

A convert asks why Catholics pray to Mary and the saints when Christ is the one mediator, and whether she must believe the messages of an approved apparition as she believes the Creed. Which answer matches the Church's teaching?

Question 9

A Catholic politician asks his bishop whether a pastoral letter criticising a specific budget as unjust binds him in conscience in the way the Church's teaching on marriage does. Which answer matches the Church's documents, and how does the same analysis apply to a law permitting abortion?

Question 10

A parish council is drafting a response to news of abuse in another diocese. One member proposes beginning with a statement of the Church's sorrow and the reforms in place, and another proposes beginning with the victims and the record. Which framework does the Church's own moral theology require, and what data should the statement include?

Question 11

Vos estis lux mundi is being discussed in a parish. One speaker says it is Church teaching that every Catholic must report abuse and that dioceses must publish the results of investigations; another says it is a procedural law whose key provision binds clerics and religious to report promptly to the local Ordinary, except for information received in the internal forum, and that it requires accessible reporting offices. Which reading matches the document?

Question 12

A teacher is asked to summarise the whole level in one framework: how does a Catholic tell the difference between what the Church has defined, what is theological opinion, what is contested, and what is mere devotion or preference? Which summary is accurate?
