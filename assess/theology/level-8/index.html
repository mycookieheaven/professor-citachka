Cumulative level test: Systematic Doctrine, Sources and Contested Cases after Chalcedon: Trinity, Christology, Atonement, the Human Person, the Last Things, and How the Church Settles or Leaves Open a Question

Question 1

A study group is asked to state, in one sentence each, two things the Church has defined about the Trinity and two things it has left open. Which set of sentences is correct?

Question 2

Three claims are made about Christ. One: 'Because the Son is one person, whatever is true of the human nature can be predicated of the divine person, so we may say that the Son of God suffered and died on the cross.' Two: 'Because the divine nature is impassible, the Son of God did not really suffer; only the man Jesus did, in whom the Word dwelt.' Three: 'Because there are two natures, there are two subjects of consciousness in Christ.' Which are Catholic and which are excluded, and by what?

Question 3

Write the correct answer to a student who asks: 'If the mechanism of the atonement is not defined, why can't I hold that Jesus's death was purely a demonstration of God's love that changes us by example, with no objective payment or satisfaction at all?'

Question 4

A teacher must explain to a class of sixteen-year-olds why the Church both accepts evolution and insists that each human soul is created immediately by God. Compose the explanation and identify what is defined, what is restricted, and what is open.

Question 5

A bereaved family asks a catechist three questions: does the Church still teach purgatory; is it true that their mother, who was not a Catholic, cannot be saved; and what will the resurrection be like? Give the catechist's answers with their levels of authority, and name one point at which the catechist must say the Church has not settled the question.

Question 6

Two Catholics are each asked to justify their reading of the creed. The first says: 'The creeds are boundary markers: each clause excludes an error, and anything the clause was not written against is left to theology.' The second says: 'The creed is a summary of the whole faith, so whatever my theology entails, the creed entails, and anything not in the creed is suspect.' Which is right, and how does the distinction apply to the filioque?

Question 7

A student preparing a paper writes: 'The Fathers are useful for background colour, but since they disagreed with each other and some were condemned, I will base my doctrine on the Catechism and skip primary sources.' Diagnose the methodological error and describe the correct use of patristic sources in a doctrinal paper.

Question 8

A diocesan official says: 'Canon 844 lets anyone in good faith receive communion at any Mass, and canon 1024 is just the current discipline about who may be ordained, both are in the Code, both can be revised.' State what is right and what is wrong, and give the test that separates the two canons.

Question 9

A journalist asks you to explain, in historically defensible terms, what the Church did in 1992 when John Paul II addressed the Galileo case. What did he say, what did he correct, and what did he not do?

Question 10

An ethics class is asked to sort three propositions: (a) 'directly targeting civilians is never permitted'; (b) 'a war may be fought only if the use of arms will not produce evils graver than the evil to be eliminated'; (c) 'economic sanctions on this state are counterproductive and should be lifted'. Which are doctrine, which is a criterion requiring prudential judgment, and which is policy?

Question 11

A seminarian is asked to write 300 words on the theme 'What the Church has defined, what it permits, and what it leaves open'. Compose the strongest version of the answer, drawing on at least three different doctrines.

Question 12

A student's essay contains this sentence: 'Because the 1995 clarification on the filioque and the 1979 letter on eschatology both acknowledge uncertainty, the Church is moving towards a looser doctrinal position on the Trinity and on hell.' Evaluate the inference and show how two different documents ought to be used.
