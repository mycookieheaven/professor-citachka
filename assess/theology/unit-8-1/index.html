Unit quiz: Doctrine and Systematic Reasoning: Trinity, Christology, Atonement, the Human Person and the Last Things

Question 1

A student summarises the Church's trinitarian doctrine as: 'There is one God, and God is three persons, so strictly speaking God is a community of three divine beings who are perfectly united in will.' Which part of that statement is defined doctrine and which part exceeds what the Church has defined?

Question 2

Two theological students disagree about Christ's human knowledge. The first says the Church has defined that Christ's human intellect enjoyed the beatific vision from the first moment of his conception, so he knew all things at all times as man. The second says that is a theological position rather than a definition, and that the question is complicated by passages such as Mark 13:32. Who has the levels right, and what does the Church actually require?

Question 3

A retreat director says: 'The good news is that Jesus did not have to die. God accepted him in place of us without any demand for payment, so the whole language of satisfaction and sacrifice is a medieval distortion.' Assess this claim against the Church's defined teaching.

Question 4

A catechist tells her class that the Church 'has never said anything about how many first parents there were, because that is a matter for science'. What is wrong with the statement, and what is the accurate three-level account of the question of human origins?

Question 5

A homilist says: 'Hell is not a place God sends anyone; it is the state of a person who has definitively refused mercy. Since God's mercy is limitless, we may be confident that in the end no one is there.' Identify precisely which parts of this are Catholic teaching and which are not.
