Unit quiz: Sources, History and Applied Ethics: Creed, Fathers, Canon Law, the Galileo Archive and the Ethics of War

Question 1

A student reads the Nicene-Constantinopolitan creed and asks whether a Catholic is bound to hold that the clause 'and from the Son' (the filioque) is part of the original creed, given that the Greek Churches do not use it. Give the accurate answer at three levels: what is defined, what is liturgically open, and what the 1995 clarification requires of Catholic presentation.

Question 2

In a debate about the concept of 'the consensus of the Fathers', one participant says the concept is useless because the Fathers contradicted each other about the soul's origin and about the fate of the unbaptised, and the Church even condemned some positions of Origen. Another says the consensus is a straightforward vote in which the majority of patristic quotations settles a question. What is the correct account of how the Church uses patristic testimony?

Question 3

A parishioner says: 'The Church changed its discipline in 2015 when it made annulments easier, which shows that its teaching on the indissolubility of marriage is really just a policy, and the same must be true of the restriction of ordination to men, since both are in the Code of Canon Law.' Diagnose the error and state what distinguishes the two cases.

Question 4

A lecturer states: 'The Galileo affair proves that the Church once taught as doctrine that the earth does not move, and that it later admitted the doctrine was false, which is why no one should take its teaching on science seriously today.' Assess the claim using the primary documents.

Question 5

A Catholic colonel argues that a proposed attack on an enemy city is proportionate because it would end the war quickly, while a Catholic peace activist argues that Pope Francis abolished the just-war criteria, so no military action can be legitimate. Using the Church's texts, show what is wrong with both arguments.
