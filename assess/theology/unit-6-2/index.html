Unit quiz: Moral Method and the Contested Cases: From the Object of the Act to a Prudential Judgement

Question 1

A surgeon proposes an operation that will foreseeably shorten a patient's life by some months but will relieve severe pain, arguing that the pain relief outweighs the lost months. A colleague objects that the Church forbids any act that shortens life. Which analysis is correct?

Question 2

A politician argues that because natural-law reasoning about the family has been used to justify unjust treatment of women and of people with same-sex attraction, the natural law is a religious imposition on a plural society and should be abandoned in public argument. Which response best reflects the Church's position as presented in this unit?

Question 3

A manager explains that she never needed to learn any moral rules, because she has a naturally generous character and simply does what a good person does; she adds that her colleague, whose upbringing was harsh, cannot be expected to behave well. Which correction reflects this unit's account of virtue?

Question 4

A hospital committee proposes to withdraw artificial nutrition and hydration from every patient with a diagnosis of a persistent vegetative state, in order to free beds, and asks a Catholic ethicist to endorse the policy as a legitimate withdrawal of disproportionate treatment. Which response is most accurate?

Question 5

In a parish discussion about a proposed immigration law, one participant says the Church teaches that restrictions on immigration are intrinsically evil, and another says the Church has no authority to say anything about borders because the question is purely political. Which evaluation is correct?
