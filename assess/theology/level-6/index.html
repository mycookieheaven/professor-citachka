Cumulative level test: Vocation, Moral Method, and the Judgements the Church Leaves Open

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Saving a lesson records reading only. Previous lesson completions have not been converted into passing scores. You can continue reading without a pass; the assessment remains unpassed.

Question 1

Two engineers describe the same factory: one describes the machines, throughput and wage schedule; the other describes the judgements the workers make, the skills they exercise, and what their employment does to their families' lives. Which description does the Church's theology of work treat as morally decisive, and why?

Question 2

A Catholic told a tribunal that her first marriage was invalid, and she now wishes to remarry in the Church. A friend objects that this is hypocrisy, since the Church says a sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved and now appears to dissolve it. How should the objection be answered?

Question 3

A retreat director describes Vatican II's teaching that the family is the domestic church and concludes that therefore every married person has a vocation to teaching the faith, so that celibate religious life is a less fruitful state than marriage. Evaluate the conclusion.

Question 4

A finance director argues that the company should reduce emissions only where doing so increases profit, since the moral obligation on a business is to its shareholders and environmental concern is a personal lifestyle matter. Using this level's material, and assuming the emission reductions involve no intrinsically disordered act, which assessment is right?

Question 5

A parish council decides to campaign for a specific housing ordinance, and a member objects that the Church has not endorsed the ordinance, so the council may not claim Catholic authority for it. How should the council proceed, on this level's account?

Question 6

An oncology team proposes to sedate a patient into unconsciousness at his request to relieve otherwise uncontrollable suffering, knowing that the sedation will likely shorten his life, and asks whether this is euthanasia. Analysing the act, which answer is correct?

Question 7

A student writes that because the natural law is grounded in what human beings naturally tend toward, and because human beings naturally tend toward sexual union and self-preservation, the natural law is simply a description of instincts and so carries no obligation. Evaluate this argument.

Question 8

A department head who has always acted fairly is suddenly placed under severe pressure and behaves worse than a colleague with a documented history of small dishonesties who is placed under comparable pressure. Does this falsify the Catholic account of virtue?

Question 9

A Catholic legislator votes for a bill that expands research using cell lines derived from embryos destroyed years ago, arguing that the bill also funds treatments for a disease that kills thousands every year. The bill contains no provision for alternative cell lines or for the registration of objection. Which evaluation follows from this level's material?

Question 10

A Catholic magazine asks a writer to defend the claim that the magisterium's teaching on the family has not changed while the pastoral practice of different dioceses plainly has. How should the writer frame the piece?

Question 11

A community group asks a parish to endorse a ballot measure on assisted suicide that criminalises assisting suicide while, in a separate clause, also restricts pain-treatment funding. Which response follows from the analysis in this unit?

Question 12

A student concludes this level by saying that the Church's teaching is useless for real decisions because it always ends in "it depends" and never tells anyone what to do. Using at least three lessons, evaluate the claim.
