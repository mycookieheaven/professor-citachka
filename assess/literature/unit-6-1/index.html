Unit quiz: Narrative time and the compressed form: pace, silence and the shape of telling

Check understanding, separately from reading

Question 1

A chapter opens with three sentences covering a family's decline over forty years and closes with one line of dialogue in which a father asks his daughter about cutting expenses. A reader claims the summarised decades are the chapter's real subject. Which assessment is best supported?

Question 2

A story narrates a party in careful scene, then jumps without comment to the characters travelling home at dawn, and ends with a paragraph describing snow while nothing further happens. How should the middle jump and the final paragraph be described?

Question 3

A student argues that Poe's insistence on unity of effect proves short stories are artistically superior to novels. Which correction is most defensible?

Question 4

An essay on idleness moves from untilled fields to unformed births to wandering minds, then reports the writer's own disappointment on retiring, and ends without resolving the question. Why is it still an essay rather than a failed argument?

Question 5

A novel's preface states that the assembled papers are contemporary records given within the range of knowledge of those who made them. The reader nevertheless concludes that no individual character could possess the novel's full knowledge. What explains the discrepancy?
