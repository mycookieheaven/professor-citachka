Cumulative level test: Cortex, wiring and modulation: from maps to habits and the appraisal of brain claims

Check understanding, separately from reading

Question 1

A paper reports that a cortical area contains a topographic map of the visual field, measured with 1 mm voxels and smoothing of 4 mm, and that the same area responds to faces. A commentary concludes that 'the face area is a map of faces'. Explain what the original measurement does and does not support, and which additional claim the commentary smuggles in.

Question 2

A researcher wants to know whether early visual deprivation has a lasting effect that a similar deprivation in adulthood would not. Design the comparison and state the two results that would distinguish a critical period from a gradually declining sensitive period.

Question 3

A dopamine neuron recorded during a task fires strongly to an unexpected reward, weakly when a fully predicted reward arrives, and dips below baseline when a predicted reward is omitted. A student concludes that the neuron codes for liking. Rebuild the interpretation and state what additional evidence the 'liking' reading would require.

Question 4

A student writes: 'Cortisol is the stress hormone, so chronic stress lowers cortisol and damages the hippocampus, and sleep disturbance is a side effect.' Correct the three claims in that sentence and state what evidence would be needed for a version that survives.

Question 5

A sleep laboratory reports that a nap in the afternoon reduces slow-wave activity in the following night, and concludes that sleep pressure is a fixed daily quantity. Evaluate that conclusion using the regulation framework and the recording-resolution logic.

Question 6

A study finds that a language task activates regions in both hemispheres, with stronger left-hemisphere response in most participants. A writer states that this confirms the left hemisphere is the language hemisphere. Identify the two errors and produce a corrected sentence.

Question 7

Two studies of the same training programme report opposite structural results: one finds a grey-matter increase in a frontal region, the other finds no change with a larger sample. Explain how both could be honest reports, and state what additional information would let you weigh them.

Question 8

A behaviour is highly practised in a stable context, continues after the outcome is devalued, and is described in the popular press as a compulsion. Reconstruct the evidence-based description and state two things the habit literature does not establish about this person.

Question 9

A headline states that a brain region was found to be 'underactive' in a group of people with a condition. Using the inference tools from this level, list the steps needed to move from that observation to a defensible claim about the condition.

Question 10

A press release for a longitudinal brain-training study says that daily practice 'rewires the adult brain permanently'. The study measured a structural image-derived difference at the end of training. Rewrite the claim as a bounded sentence and list the two pieces of missing evidence you would request.

Question 11

You are asked to referee a manuscript claiming that a critical period for a cognitive skill closes at age 7, based on a cross-sectional comparison of performance in three age groups. Write the three most important revisions you would require and justify each.

Question 12

Assemble the full appraisal of the claim that acquiring the layout of a large city enlarges the hippocampus. State the design that supports the strongest version, the design that would still be needed for a general claim about learning, and one sentence on how the popular version should be corrected.
