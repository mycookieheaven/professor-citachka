Cumulative level test: Beyond the point neuron: circuit mechanisms beneath behaviour and the methods that can test them

Question 1

A paper claims that a class of neurons performs linear integration, based on a regression of somatic voltage on the number of stimulated spines with a slope of 0.9 millivolts per spine and an intercept of 2 millivolts, fitted across 12 stimulation intensities. You have one extra recording session. Which single manipulation would most directly test the linearity claim, and what would the result look like under a nonlinear mechanism?

Question 2

Two reviews of the same astrocyte case make opposite recommendations: one concludes that astrocytes are integral synaptic elements, the other that the key release evidence is unreliable. A researcher must decide whether to spend a year on a follow-up experiment. Using the two positions, what design would produce a result that both reviews would have to accept?

Question 3

You are editing a textbook chapter that currently states that critical periods end when perineuronal nets form, and that adult brains therefore cannot change with experience. Two sentences need rewriting. Which pair of sentences is defensible?

Question 4

A funding panel receives two proposals about adult human hippocampal neurogenesis. Proposal one will count doublecortin-positive cells in autopsy tissue from 40 donors aged 20 to 80 using one antibody and existing protocols. Proposal two will process surgical and autopsy tissue from the same donors through two protocols, use two immaturity markers plus morphology, and pre-register the counting rules. Which should the panel prefer, and on what reasoning?

Question 5

A pharmaceutical company proposes to develop a compound that enhances microglial synaptic pruning during adolescence, on the grounds that microglia prune synapses in development and that altered pruning is implicated in psychiatric disorders. As a scientific adviser, what is your primary objection and what evidence would be required to overcome it?

Question 6

A preprint reports that a model's middle layer best matches a cortical area, with a correlation of 0.5 that was estimated after averaging 200 trials per participant across 15 participants, and it argues that the correspondence explains the area's function. Meanwhile a recording paper reports that only 40 per cent of its sorted units met an isolation threshold. How do the two reporting problems belong to the same family?

Question 7

A study reports that a polygenic score for a behavioural trait correlates with a brain imaging measure at r equal to 0.15 in 8,000 participants, that the imaging measure correlates with the behaviour at r equal to 0.2, and concludes that the imaging measure mediates the genetic influence. Which elements of this argument are quantitatively and conceptually weak?

Question 8

You have one month and a fixed budget to test whether a specific cell population is necessary for a behaviour. Choose between a chemogenetic suppression study with 30 animals per group and an optogenetic suppression study with 15 animals per group performed within subjects, and justify the choice with an explicit account of the artifacts each design must bound.

Question 9

Two years from now, a laboratory publishes a study of adult human hippocampal neurogenesis using tissue from the same donors analysed through both a fresh frozen protocol and a standard fixed protocol, with two immaturity markers, morphology criteria and pre-registered counting rules. The result is that the fixed protocol detects almost no young neurons while the fresh frozen protocol detects a small number. What is the correct interpretation, and what would remain unresolved?

Question 10

A theory paper argues that a specific dendritic nonlinearity implements a Bayesian inference scheme, showing that a model with this nonlinearity reproduces three published sets of findings. Which additions would turn this into a constraining claim rather than a description, and how would you test them?

Question 11

Compare two mechanistic claims: that a dendritic plateau potential depolarises the soma enough to influence spike output, and that an astrocyte calcium microdomain triggers gliotransmitter release that changes a synapse. Both rest on measured signals and both involve a step from signal to effect. What distinguishes their evidential standing, and what does each need to be comparable?

Question 12

A trainee proposes a study with these features: 12 animals per group, optogenetic activation of a projection at 25 milliwatts, no sham illumination group, chemogenetic suppression at 10 milligrams per kilogram of ligand with vehicle controls only, one anatomical marker for cell identity, and analysis of 14 behavioural outcomes with the significant ones reported. Reprioritise the study's resources and give the three changes that would most improve what it can conclude.
