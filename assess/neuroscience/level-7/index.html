Cumulative level test: Change under scrutiny: how learning, development, ageing and interventions change the brain, and how such claims are tested

Question 1

A laboratory reports that a brief visuomotor rotation training session changed the direction-tuning curves of a small set of motor cortex neurons, and a commentary concludes that the rotation task's internal model is encoded in those neurons. A second laboratory reports that after the same rotation training, a transcranial magnetic stimulation protocol that transiently disrupts the same region abolishes the after-effect for about twenty minutes and then the after-effect returns. Explain what each result supports, why the second is not a refutation of the first, and what further evidence the encoding claim requires.

Question 2

A cognitive-training company cites three studies: a randomised trial showing improved performance on the trained task; a cross-sectional study showing that long-term users of the product have different brain activity from non-users; and a study showing that a subgroup of high responders improved on an untrained memory test after a further analysis. You must explain, for each, what it can support and what single design change would make it more probative, and then state the overall evidential position carefully.

Question 3

A school considers a policy based on the claim that learning a second language must begin before age seven. Supporting evidence is offered in three pieces: a study in which late learners score lower on average on a grammaticality test that has a maximum score reached by many early learners; a reanalysis showing a significant quadratic term for age of onset in a regression; and an animal study on monocular deprivation. For each piece, state what it supports, and give the overall verdict on the policy premise.

Question 4

A research team tests the dual-systems account of adolescent risk taking with three analyses on one dataset of 60 adolescents: a cross-sectional correlation between a reward-reactivity index and risk taking, a comparison of mean reward reactivity between two age groups, and a median split on an imbalance score with a t-test between the resulting subgroups. Evaluate each analysis and state what a within-person test would require.

Question 5

A memory clinic advertises a screening package using three tests, each with reported sensitivity around 85 per cent and specificity around 85 per cent, and states that a positive result on any one test indicates early impairment. A patient scores below the cut-off on one test. Using the reasoning of this level, explain how the any-one-test rule changes the properties of the package, what would be required to interpret the result for this person, and why a single score cannot bear the claim.

Question 6

You are asked to review a manuscript claiming that a drug given after retrieval erases a fear memory in humans. The manuscript reports a reduction in startle potentiation at 24 hours, no manipulation check on the drug's physiological effect, no measure of whether the reminder produced prediction error, an administered-versus-placebo comparison with randomisation but no blinding of participants, and a secondary analysis showing the effect in participants who reported being surprised by the reminder. Explain which of these features undermine the erasure claim and how, and what the surprise subgroup analysis does and does not add.

Question 7

A workplace introduces a policy of banning concurrent task performance, justified by three claims: that switching between tasks costs a fixed percentage of productive time, that high perceptual load eliminates distractibility, and that multitasking habits reduce attentional control. For each claim, state the evidence standard it would need and evaluate what the literature in this level actually provides.

Question 8

A behaviour-change programme claims that a new routine will 'rewire' a habit within three weeks and that the routine will then persist regardless of whether it still helps. Evaluate both parts of the claim using the tests and evidence in this level, and describe the design that would test the persistence part.

Question 9

A trial reports that a twelve-week programme improved a laboratory measure of attention in older adults by 0.3 standard deviations compared with a no-contact control, that expectations were higher in the programme arm, and that no differences were found on self-reported everyday function. A commentary calls the result a proven cognitive benefit. Explain what each element of the trial does to the claim, compute what the numbers imply about scale and power, and state the verdict.

Question 10

A product's evidence package contains four items: a randomised trial of the product versus an active control published by the company's own researchers, showing no far transfer and a significant near-transfer effect; a mechanism paper showing that training changes activity in a task-relevant region; a peer-reviewed study showing a correlation between hours of use and self-rated sharpness; and a ten-year randomised trial of a different training format showing durable gains on multi-ability test composites. Write the calibrated verdict, stating for each item what it supports and naming the two findings that would most change the verdict.

Question 11

A study in healthy older adults reports that six weeks of cognitive training improved a composite memory score by 0.25 standard deviations relative to an active control, and that a structural imaging measure of hippocampal volume increased slightly in the training arm. A commentary concludes that training protects the brain against ageing. Explain what the two results can and cannot support, how a surrogate-endpoint argument bears on the claim, and what would be needed for the protection claim.

Question 12

You must design one study that could, in principle, distinguish three explanations for why adolescent musicians perform better than non-musicians on an attention task: training-induced change, self-selection of people with better attention into music, and expectancy or motivation during testing. State the design, the measurement plan, and how each of the three explanations would be separated.
