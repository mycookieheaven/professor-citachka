Question 1

A fictional man of 51 is brought to an emergency department on day four after stopping heavy drinking, with fever, sweating, disorientation, agitation and visual hallucinations. A colleague records 'first episode of psychosis, commence antipsychotic'. Using the symptom-strategy distinction, the withdrawal evidence and the timeline discipline, write the correction you would make. Which longest-form answer is strongest?

Question 2

A fictional woman of 29 is described as having 'suspected emotionally unstable personality disorder' after three months of intense relationships, one episode of self-harm, and a recent separation. She has no history of such difficulties before this year. Which answer best applies the threshold logic and the language discipline together?

Question 3

A fictional 41-year-old refers himself for 'burnout'. His account: two years of needing to recover in a dark room after any social event, lifelong difficulty telling whether a remark was hostile, one intense interest since childhood, and repeated sick leave since a restructuring. He says school was fine because his mother managed everything. He denies low mood. Which answer best integrates the developmental and the alternative-explanation reasoning?

Question 4

A fictional 68-year-old is described by his daughter as 'inflexible and unable to cope' since his wife died. He reports the same rigid daily order for forty years, two close friends, and distress whenever plans change, which she confirms was always true. A week's screening instrument rates him 'low risk, no follow-up'. Two errors are in play. Which answer identifies both?

Question 5

A fictional client describes a violent assault. She gives one detailed sensory fragment, reports a gap in the sequence, and says she fears she is inventing it. Different staff ask her to retell it on four occasions; the account becomes more detailed each time. A colleague writes 'detail increasing under questioning, query fabrication'. Which response is most defensible?

Question 6

A fictional service wants to 'risk stratify' all new referrals, arguing that stratification allows scarce psychological therapy places to go to the most urgent cases and that no alternative exists. Using the guideline, the rationale document and the base-rate arithmetic, which answer is strongest?

Question 7

A fictional headline reads: 'Therapy halves relapse, major trial shows.' The linked paper is registered, but its reported primary outcome differs from the registered one, dropouts are 9 per cent in the treatment arm and 27 per cent in the control arm, and the confidence interval around the effect is wide. Which appraisal is most defensible?

Question 8

A fictional review of therapy for depression pools published trials and reports a moderate effect. A trainee proposes checking no further, because the studies were peer reviewed. Using the evidence on publication bias and the appraisal sequence, which answer is strongest?

Question 9

A fictional person in rehabilitation says he wants two mornings of work a week, not fewer symptoms. His care coordinator objects that work will destabilise him and proposes to wait for a further six months of stability. Which answer best applies rehabilitation and recovery reasoning?

Question 10

A fictional ward round records a person as 'no insight, difficult, frequently requests PRN'. A week later she is discharged, and the summary repeats 'no insight'. Nothing records why she was requesting medication or what she said about it. Which answer names the accuracy and care failures and the practical remedy?

Question 11

A fictional 22-year-old presents with agitation, unusual beliefs about neighbours, and heavy cannabis use for two months, after a job loss. His brother reports a similar two-week episode at 19 during a period of heavy use that resolved. Which reasoning is strongest across the timeline, developmental and formulation skills of this level?

Question 12

A fictional department proposes a quality dashboard built from four indicators: mean symptom score, number of risk assessments completed with a stratified band, mean therapist-rated improvement, and percentage of cases closed within twelve weeks. Using the reasoning of this level, which answer best describes the problem?
