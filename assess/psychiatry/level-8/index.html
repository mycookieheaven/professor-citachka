Cumulative level test: From lived experience to service systems: how psychiatric knowledge is made, measured and contested

Question 1

A fictional research team reports that a five-item experience scale has internal consistency of 0.91, test-retest reliability of 0.85, and a factor structure matching its published form. Their paper proposes the scale as a clinical screening tool for a specific perceptual anomaly. Using this level's treatment of description, instruments and measurement, what has the team established and what has it not?

Question 2

A fictional early-intervention service proposes to use a phenomenological instrument to decide who receives a preventive intervention, citing a follow-up study in a high-risk sample in which the instrument's total score predicted shorter time to onset in a Cox model controlling for other predictors, and a diagnostic study in which scores aggregated in one spectrum. Write the reasoning that should decide whether the service can do this.

Question 3

A fictional guidelines committee is choosing between a long-established diagnostic category and a proposed replacement. Evidence presented for the replacement: better agreement between raters (kappa 0.62 versus 0.45 under the study's own design), narrower criteria, and a supporting narrative from clinical experts. Evidence against: no follow-up or family study has compared the two. What should the committee's reasoning be?

Question 4

A fictional trainee drafts a paragraph: 'Three meta-analyses report that this intervention works, with pooled effects of 0.4 to 0.6. Because synthesis is at the top of the evidence hierarchy, the intervention is established.' Rewrite the appraisal in this level's terms, naming the checks that determine how much the pooled figure can bear.

Question 5

A fictional guideline recommends an intervention on the basis of a 20% relative reduction in a serious event, and a service with a caseload spanning a wide range of baseline risks asks how to describe the benefit to two different people: one with an estimated two-year event risk of 50%, the other 5%. Compute the absolute effects and state what follows for how the recommendation should be presented.

Question 6

A fictional formulary paper concludes: 'Certainty of evidence is low for this outcome, so the intervention does not work for it.' Which reformulation is correct, and what should the certainty rating have been doing?

Question 7

A fictional service decides to adopt a therapy whose trials reported an effect size of 0.7 against waiting list. Six months after adoption, routine outcomes show no improvement relative to the service's previous year. List the explanations this level's evidence would put in order, and say what each implies for what to measure next.

Question 8

A fictional region has reduced inpatient beds by 15% and increased community team capacity. Two years later, admissions are down 18%, out-of-area placements are up 40%, and the number of people in contact with services is unchanged. Write the systems reading of those three numbers and the one measurement that would most clarify them.

Question 9

You are asked to appraise: 'A randomised trial of 300 patients found that a structured intervention reduced symptom scores by 4 points more than treatment as usual; the developers, who ran the trial, report that the difference exceeded the scale's minimal important difference; the intervention is recommended for routine use.' Identify the four decisive checks and the one that most changes the conclusion.

Question 10

Two fictional studies of the same diagnostic interview report agreement of 0.78 and 0.34. The second study used the same interview but had the second assessment conducted by a fresh interviewer without access to the first set of notes. A guideline developer asks which figure to use. What reasoning does this level supply, and what else about the interview would you want to know?

Question 11

A fictional clinician reads two reports of the same person's experience. The first records: 'Patient reports that thoughts are inserted; no insight.' The second records: 'Says thoughts arrive already spoken, in a voice that is not his, though he says they come from his own head; when asked whether that makes the thoughts his, says he does not know what would make a thought his.' Using this level's material, assess the two records and what each can support.

Question 12

Assemble the reasoning for this decision: a fictional service with 4,000 people on its caseload wants to commission a new intensive intervention based on one positive randomised trial conducted in a different country, and the trial's own service had one trained clinician per 40 patients while this service has one per 120. Write the four-part assessment and the single cheapest measurement that would most reduce the uncertainty.
