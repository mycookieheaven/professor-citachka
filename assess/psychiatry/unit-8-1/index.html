Question 1

A fictional supervisor reads a first-assessment note that says: 'Patient reports feeling detached; endorses low mood; denies plans. Impression: depersonalisation.' The interview transcript shows she said 'my arms move before I decide to move them, and I watch it happen as if it were a video of someone else'. Using lesson 71's method, what is the strongest criticism of the note?

Question 2

A fictional research meeting discusses a proposal to screen all first-episode referrals with a self-disorder interview, on the grounds that the construct aggregates in the schizophrenia spectrum and predicted transition in an ultra-high-risk cohort. A statistician asks what would be needed before it could guide a service. Which answer is best supported by the cited literature?

Question 3

A fictional service compares mean depression scores across two language versions of the same scale and announces that speakers of language B are more distressed, because their mean is two points higher. A colleague asks what would make that inference safe. What does lesson 73 require?

Question 4

A fictional committee is asked to decide whether to adopt a proposed refinement of an existing diagnosis. The evidence presented is that the refined criteria identify a more homogeneous group in the authors' sample, and that kappa agreement between raters using the refinement was 0.71. Which is the missing element a revising body would expect?

Question 5

A fictional clinician reads two summaries of the same intervention: a 2011 review reporting a moderate pooled effect, and a 2019 update of the same literature reporting a smaller effect, noting that the earlier pooled estimate included several trials that had not been prospectively registered. What is the most defensible interpretation, and what would she look for next?
