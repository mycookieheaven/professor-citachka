Cumulative level test: The interview as an instrument: elicitation, observation, translation and honest documents

Check understanding, separately from reading

Question 1

A fictional psychiatrist is asked to assess a man on a busy ward at 1 a.m., thirty-six hours after admission, whose speech is rapid and who keeps returning to the same grievance. She has twenty minutes. Which approach follows this level's teaching about interview structure and question form?

Question 2

A fictional note reads: 'Appeared agitated and guarded. Denied any problems at home. Reported that his wife was "fine about everything". Affect was flat and inappropriate.' Which statement best applies this level's discipline about reported and observed material?

Question 3

A fictional service administers a self-report symptom questionnaire to a woman with limited English, and an interpreter who knows her family translates the items aloud, explaining them in his own words. The score is recorded and used in the formulation. What is the main problem?

Question 4

A fictional team meeting concludes that a man scores above a questionnaire's cut-off, and the entry in his record states that he therefore 'has a moderate depressive episode'. Which criticism is correct?

Question 5

A fictional service considers using a screening questionnaire with a sensitivity of about 90 per cent and a specificity of about 90 per cent. In a first-contact population where roughly 1 in 100 people has the condition being screened for, what does the arithmetic imply about positive results?

Question 6

A fictional team is asked to introduce a stratified risk-category system after a serious incident, with every person given a low, medium or high label at the end of assessment. A senior clinician objects. Which objection is best supported by the guidance cited in this level?

Question 7

A fictional trainee reads that poor insight is associated with poorer treatment adherence and that good insight is associated with depression and lower self-reported quality of life, and concludes that the service should stop pushing people to recognise their difficulties. What is the error?

Question 8

A fictional service's leaflet states that people who fall below the assessment threshold 'do not have a mental health problem and should access self-help resources'. Using this level's material, what is the objection?

Question 9

A fictional 34-year-old man attends with low mood, poor sleep and irritability and reports drinking heavily most nights. The assessing clinician writes that the depression is primary and the drinking is a consequence, and arranges treatment for depression. Using this level's material, what is the main difficulty?

Question 10

A fictional assessment document is complete, well organised and accurate, but it lists only the areas that were covered in the appointment. Later readers cannot tell whether substance use, developmental history and current risk were asked about and denied, or never asked. Which repair follows from this level's material on documenting uncertainty?

Question 11

A fictional team assesses a man who speaks little English and who has been described in his referral as 'vague and evasive'. The assessment is conducted with an interpreter over twenty-five minutes, and the trainee finds the material confusing. Which combination of this level's ideas is the best response?

Question 12

A fictional 30-year-old woman declines an offered treatment. Her clinician records 'lacks insight into the need for treatment; not competent to decide'. Within the same week, the same woman gives a clear and consistent account of her finances, her housing and her reasons for refusing, and asks for a different treatment to be considered. Using this level's material, which statement is correct?
