Cumulative level test: Law, specialised surfaces and clinical boundaries: deciding what a cosmetic can answer

Check understanding, separately from reading

Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Question 1

A learner says she only buys cosmetics that are 'FDA approved'. Which statement correctly describes what United States law actually provides for a moisturiser?

Question 2

A device sold online is described as 'FDA registered' and 'clinically proven to boost collagen'. It is a 1.5 mm microneedling roller for home use. What is the accurate assessment?

Question 3

A learner compares a European sunscreen labelled SPF 50+ with a United States product labelled SPF 100 and assumes the American product offers twice the protection. What is the sound correction?

Question 4

A learner notices that her American sunscreen contains zinc oxide and titanium dioxide while her European one lists bemotrizinol and drometrizole trisiloxane. She concludes the American product is the safer 'physical' option. How should this be assessed?

Question 5

A learner develops nail-fold itching, two lifting nail plates and eyelid eczema after switching from lacquer to UV-cured gel. She has a dental appointment for a composite filling next month. What should the appraisal include?

Question 6

A learner's lower lip has had a crusted, non-healing area for eight weeks. She has been exfoliating it with a sugar scrub and applying a peppermint balm. What is the correct response?

Question 7

A learner reports itching that began three months ago, is generalised, disturbs sleep and is accompanied by unintentional weight loss. She has no rash. What is the correct action?

Question 8

A learner reads that a 'hair growth serum' contains caffeine, biotin and peptides, and that a randomised trial of a low-level laser comb increased terminal hair density. She has patterned thinning at the temples. What is the accurate appraisal?

Question 9

A learner asks why her dermatologist wants to look at her scalp with a magnifier before deciding about treatment, when the shedding has already been going on for four months. What is the best explanation?

Question 10

A learner has a single dark patch on the cheek that appeared after a healed spot, and a friend tells her it is melasma and that she needs prescription creams. What is the correct appraisal?

Question 11

A learner taking amiodarone develops a burning erythema on the face and hands after a bright weekend, and a friend advises her to stop the tablet until the skin settles. What is the correct response?

Question 12

A learner wants to test whether a retinoid improves the texture of her right cheek. She plans four two-week periods, alternating the product and a plain moisturiser, with a one-week gap, photographs taken by a friend who does not know the schedule, and a decision rule written down beforehand. What is the main remaining threat to her conclusion?
