Cumulative level test: Appraising and applying skin evidence: measurement, trials and formulation decisions

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. Question 1

Cumulative application. A brand presents a barrier claim as follows: trans-epidermal water loss fell 22 per cent in a four-week study of 28 participants using the product, measured on the forearm with an open-chamber device, and the same product is recommended on its website for use around the eyes. A learner with reactive cheeks and a diagnosed corneal condition asks whether to buy it. Build the appraisal in the order this level teaches, naming the site, the instrument, the design and the boundary. State what the 22 per cent does and does not establish, and state what you would do about the eye-area recommendation.

Question 2

Cumulative application. Two colleagues present evidence for the same barrier cream. Colleague one cites a study in which capacitance readings rose from 42 to 51 arbitrary units after eight weeks in a single treated group. Colleague two cites a regional measurement study reporting that TEWL reliability ranges from 0.74 to 0.4 across sites while capacitance reliability was above 0.75 everywhere. Colleague one concludes the cream works and colleague two says the number cannot be interpreted. Using instrument and design reasoning, adjudicate, and include the arithmetic that converts the change into units and compares it with an illustrative measurement error.

Question 3

Cumulative application. A cosmetic trial reports that 60 participants were randomised to active or vehicle, that 48 completed, and that the active group improved significantly in the per-protocol analysis of completers. The abstract says the treatment was proven effective. Identify the specific reporting and analysis problems, state what the intention-to-treat analysis would require, and name the two checklist items a reader should look for that this summary omits.

Question 4

Cumulative application. A 40-participant split-face trial finds no significant difference between the treated and control sides at twelve weeks, and the brand argues that the null result nevertheless shows the design was rigorous. A sceptical reader argues the null result proves the product does not work. Using the design logic from this level, state the correct interpretation and explain what further information would change the conclusion.

Question 5

Cumulative application. A learner finds three sources on one question: a manufacturer summary reporting a 30 per cent wrinkle improvement, a network meta-analysis ranking retinoids highest among topical agents with a stated safety caveat, and a systematic review noting spin in abstracts of dermatology meta-analyses. Explain how to weigh the three, what the 30 per cent needs before it can be compared with the network meta-analysis, and what the spin study changes about how the learner should read the other two.

Question 6

Cumulative application. A learner with a diagnosed corneal condition and impaired vision wants to try a new under-eye cream marketed as ophthalmologist tested, and asks whether she should also resume applying eyeliner at the inner lash line because a video showed it makes eyes look larger. Using the periorbital evidence, answer both parts, name the mechanism by which the eyeliner change matters, and state the stop rule that should accompany any decision.

Question 7

Cumulative application. A dermatologist recommends an occlusive ointment for lichenified skin on a shin. The learner dislikes it and asks whether the lighter lotion she prefers delivers the same active, since the percentage on the label is identical. Explain what the vehicle changes, give the site-based reasoning, and include the arithmetic that converts measured delivery into a share of the applied dose.

Question 8

Cumulative application. A learner with a history of a rash from a scented body lotion wants to eliminate risk by choosing fragrance-free and paraben-free products, and she wants to know whether that plan is sound. Evaluate the plan using hazard and risk reasoning, the preservative patch-test frequencies, the fragrance labelling rules and the contamination argument.

Question 9

Cumulative application. A brand's serum claims to deliver a growth-factor complex that rebuilds collagen, and the cited study measured wrinkle depth at eight weeks in 45 participants with no control group, reporting a 12 per cent relative improvement. A second brand sells a much cheaper matrikine peptide serum and cites a review of products with evidence of skin rejuvenation. Compare the two claims on outcome evidence and mechanism, include the arithmetic, and state what would move either claim into the likely category.

Question 10

Cumulative application. A learner wants to buy a microbiome-balancing serum, has 300 dollars a year to spend on skincare, and also reports recurrent stinging on the cheeks and a persistent dry patch on a shin that has not healed in three months. Build the priorities she should follow, including which question is clinical, which interventions have the best evidence behind them, and how to reason about the microbiome claim.

Question 11

Cumulative application. A learner is building her first dossier and asks you to check the seven sections. She has a question, nine sources including four manufacturer brochures, a summary paragraph, a conclusion that the product is worth trying, and a note saying she will stop if it irritates her skin. Diagnose what is missing, state the criteria that should determine her certainty label, and explain the two kinds of stop rule the dossier needs.

Question 12

Cumulative synthesis. A learner presents a plan that uses four products, including a peptide serum, a microbiome serum, an acid exfoliant and an eye area cream applied to the lid margin, justifies each with a mechanism, and reports that she cannot afford a sunscreen. Rebuild the plan using the evidence hierarchy from this level, name the arithmetic that shows where the money should go, and state precisely how the eye-area item changes and why.
