Cumulative level test: Skin across a lifetime: hormonal context, skin of colour, working life, and reading products honestly

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Question 1

A 62-year-old asks you to rank four products that promise to reverse thinning, and mentions that since November the same products sting, their shins crack, and the house is heated all day. Separately, a friend says a humidifier and cooler showers fixed the same complaint. Construct the reasoning that decides what to do first.

Question 2

A 51-year-old has itchy, dry, sometimes eczematous skin that began when her cycles became irregular. She has used the same fragranced body wash for ten years, has recently started a new laundry detergent, and asks about a phytoestrogen serum. Design the reasoning and the observation plan.

Question 3

A learner with deep brown skin presents three problems: flat brown marks from old acne, acne that still flares, and a raised firm nodule at an old piercing site that sometimes itches. She asks which of two serums to buy and whether to scrub first. Build the full plan and justify each element.

Question 4

A dishwasher in a restaurant washes their hands about fifty times a shift, wears gloves for long unbroken periods, and has deep painful cracks. A landscape gardener colleague burns on cloudy days and takes a diuretic. Compare the two exposure problems and set out the actions that follow for each.

Question 5

A learner reports burning within seconds of applying most products, no visible change, and a shelf of eleven items. Three weeks later the same learner reports a red, scaly rash spreading from the jaw to the cheeks that began after a new hair dye. Explain how one case becomes two and what each requires.

Question 6

A learner uses a borrowed unlabelled cream for facial redness and notices rapid improvement, thinning skin, new visible vessels and, on any attempt to stop, more redness than before. Their clinician had separately prescribed a treatment for a diagnosed condition, which the learner has been alternating with the borrowed cream. Set out the reasoning and the obligations.

Question 7

A learner compares two sunscreens. One is a cosmetic-only moisturiser with 'with vitamin C' and a long ingredient list; the other carries an SPF 50 claim, a drug facts panel and a water-resistance statement. They also want to know how much to use. Explain how to read both packages and how to convert the protection claim into a dose.

Question 8

Three advertisements arrive in one week: one reports that 94 percent of 100 participants agreed their skin looked smoother, one reports a study of 32 participants over 8 weeks against a vehicle, and one says dermatologist tested, hypoallergenic, clinically proven. Rank what can be concluded and design the purchase decision that follows.

Question 9

Build one routine for a 34-year-old outdoor worker with reactive dry skin, a twelve-dollar monthly budget, four minutes each morning and wet hands every evening, and state what would have to be true for a fourth step to be added.

Question 10

A learner in a hot, humid city notices oiliness, breakouts on the back and a wet face after a daily cycle commute, and decides to drop sunscreen and add an exfoliant and a heavier night cream. Explain what the climate actually changes and what it does not change.

Question 11

A learner in their 60s is proud of a nine-step routine, has a new dark spot on the cheek that they cover with concealer, has not had a skin examination, and reports that a moisturiser marketed for mature skin has made their skin sting. Assemble the review of this whole situation.

Question 12

A learner asks you to combine everything from this level into one decision about a single new product they want to buy: it is expensive, sold through an influencer who demonstrates results, claims to treat acne marks and uneven tone, comes in a package whose ingredient list is partly illegible, and the learner also has active acne that is starting to scar. Set out the complete reasoning.
