Cumulative level test: Mechanism-led skincare: barrier repair, photoprotection, and active-ingredient control

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Saving a lesson records reading only. Previous lesson completions have not been converted into passing scores. You can continue reading without a pass; the assessment remains unpassed.

Question 1

A learner presents three facts about their own routine: tightness lasting twenty minutes after a strong foaming cleanser, a light lotion applied as a pea-sized dot, and a new decision to add an exfoliating acid twice weekly to smooth the flaking. Sequence the reasoning and choose the correct first action, stating which barrier component each fact implicates.

Question 2

Two learners compare strategies. The first applies a light lotion generously over the whole body after every shower. The second applies an occlusive ointment only to the palms, heels, and shins. Using the tested dose of 2 milligrams per square centimetre and a body surface area of about 1.8 square metres, explain which approach is more defensible and why the site choice matters more than the product's elegance.

Question 3

A learner has brown marks on both cheeks from resolved acne, reports that the marks darken every summer, and asks whether to start a strong exfoliating routine to speed fading. Explain why the routine is the wrong instrument, identify the two interventions that come first, and state the realistic timeframe and its anatomical dependence.

Question 4

A learner with active acne and residual marks plans four simultaneous changes: a nightly retinol, an exfoliating acid every other night, a stronger cleanser, and a new brightening serum, with the objective of clearing both the lesions and the marks by a wedding in six weeks. Evaluate the plan against the mechanisms in this level and propose an ordered alternative.

Question 5

A learner's cream is described on its page as hypoallergenic, dermatologist tested, and formulated without the twenty most common allergens, and the learner developed an itchy rash on the face two days after starting it that also affected the eyelids. Explain what each label statement does and does not establish, and describe the correct next step.

Question 6

A learner wants to use a retinol product alongside a weekly exfoliating acid, a foaming cleanser, and a fragranced toner, and asks how to increase the retinol to nightly as fast as possible in order to see results sooner. Explain the irritation budget, the timing of benefit, and what the stopping rule should contain before any escalation.

Question 7

A learner is choosing between two products for year-round daily commuting: an SPF 70 lotion whose label gives no UVA information, and a broad-spectrum SPF 30 fluid that they find pleasant enough to reapply at midday. Construct the argument that decides the choice using the properties of each labelled number and the physics of the two ultraviolet ranges.

Question 8

A learner applies a humectant serum twice daily, notices softness that disappears by mid-afternoon in a heated flat, and reports that a heavy ointment works everywhere except the groin and underarms, where it feels intolerable. Explain both observations and specify the change that follows from each.

Question 9

A learner applies a tinted SPF 20 base each morning because it is the only product they will wear, spends ninety minutes driving with sun through the side window, and never reapplies. Using the dose arithmetic, the spectral argument, and the reapplication logic, explain what their actual protection is likely to be and which change yields the largest real-world gain.

Question 10

Over one week a learner reports three changes: immediate stinging and redness after reusing a strong acid, a patch of itchy scaly rash appearing two days after a new shampoo and spreading beyond the hairline, and several new tender bumps on the jaw where they have been picking. Sort the three, state what evidence justifies each classification, and identify which one requires clinical referral and why.

Question 11

A product page lists five statements: cosmetic facility registration, clinically proven to reduce wrinkles, pharmaceutical grade retinol at 2 percent, oil-free and non-comedogenic, and dermatologist tested. For each, state what standard or evidence would be required for it to function as information rather than persuasion, and identify which statements are regulated categories at all.

Question 12

A learner has oily, easily irritated skin, active acne on the chin, dark marks from old lesions, two failed attempts at high-strength actives, and a strong preference for a short routine they will actually follow. Construct a six-week sequence that respects the mechanisms in this level, stating the order of operations, what is explicitly excluded, the single change introduced at each stage, and the stopping rule that governs the whole plan.
