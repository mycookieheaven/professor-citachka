Question 1

A fictional practice runs a Saturday vaccine clinic where owners are offered whatever the reminder card says, without any history questions. A four month old puppy arrives with a card showing doses at six and ten weeks and the owner says the breeder told her the puppy is now fully protected. Which statement best evaluates the practice and the owner's belief?

Question 2

A fictional household has three dogs and one cat. The dogs are treated for fleas monthly and the cat is not treated because she stays indoors, and the dogs are shampooed weekly. The owner reports that the dogs still scratch and has brought in a stool sample from one dog that is positive for roundworm. Which analysis is best supported?

Question 3

A fictional twelve year old cat is presented for a routine visit. The technician records a weight of four point two kilograms against four point six kilograms a year earlier, a body condition score of four out of nine, and leaves the muscle condition score blank with a note that the cat was difficult to handle. The veterinarian asks whether the weight loss is likely to be significant. Which response best reflects the guidelines and the technician's role?

Question 4

A fictional client declines a dental procedure for her dog for the third consecutive year, saying the dog eats well and the smell is just what dogs have. The technician has noted generalised gingival redness and calculus on the left side, and the veterinarian has recommended assessment under anaesthesia with radiographs. Which statement best represents accurate reasoning for the technician to offer?

Question 5

A fictional technician is asked to complete a nail trim on a dog who is rigid, wide eyed, closed mouthed and motionless, and who allowed the previous two procedures without protest. A colleague suggests a firm hold because the dog is being good. Which evaluation is best supported by the evidence?
