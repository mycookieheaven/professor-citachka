Cumulative level test: Support Under Pressure: Oxygen, Perfusion, Metabolism and the Care That Continues

Question 1

A fictional 7 kg cat with a urethral obstruction arrives with a potassium of 8.1 mEq/L and a heart rate of 90 beats per minute with poor pulse quality. The technician connects the ECG, places the patient on oxygen and prepares the catheter trolley. Explain what the ECG is being used to detect, why the potassium result needs a handling check before it is treated as real, and who decides the interventions.

Question 2

A fictional 22 kg dog with a perforated intestine is four hours post-operative. Blood pressure is 78/40 mm Hg with a mean of 52 mm Hg, lactate is 4.6 mmol/L, urine output is 0.3 mL/kg/h, and the dog is quiet but responsive. A colleague describes the patient as 'stable because the temperature is normal'. Build the argument that corrects this description, using the sepsis consensus material.

Question 3

A fictional practice measures blood pressure in every cat over nine years of age using an oscillometric device, one measurement, with the cuff on the tail while the cat is held in lateral recumbency by two people. A 12-year-old cat reads 185 mm Hg systolic and the veterinarian is asked to start treatment. Identify the three protocol failures and state what the ACVIM statement requires before a diagnosis.

Question 4

A fictional 3.6 kg cat with a newly diagnosed diabetic crisis has a blood glucose of 486 mg/dL, venous pH 7.21, beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.8 mmol/L and potassium 3.2 mEq/L. The veterinarian starts an insulin infusion. List the four variables the technician should be measuring, with the interval and the reason for each.

Question 5

A fictional 6 kg cat with pancreatitis has not eaten for four days and vomits when fed by mouth. Compare a naso-oesophageal tube with an oesophagostomy tube for this patient, naming one advantage, one contraindication and one daily maintenance task for each, and state the caloric starting point.

Question 6

A fictional 5 kg cat is admitted with a blocked urethra. The technician is asked to prepare the catheterisation trolley and the monitoring sheet. Describe the aseptic and system choices that matter, one piece of evidence about closed systems, and the two urine volumes that would make the technician escalate.

Question 7

A fictional 30 kg dog is recumbent after a fibrocartilaginous embolus. It is incontinent of urine, cannot turn itself, and has a dry cornea on the dependent eye. Build a nursing plan with four timed elements and the physiological reason for each.

Question 8

A fictional client must give subcutaneous fluids to a 4 kg cat at home twice weekly. The veterinarian has prescribed the volume. The technician has ten minutes at discharge. Design the teaching sequence and name two things that must be documented.

Question 9

A fictional 11-year-old Labrador is euthanased at home. The owner asks whether the eyes will stay open, whether the body can be buried in the garden, and how the technician knows the dog has died. Answer all three, naming the limits of each answer.

Question 10

A fictional 8 kg pug with brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome is recovering from anaesthesia. The technician must choose between flow-by oxygen, a facemask and a nasal line, and must state what would make each choice wrong. Construct the comparison.

Question 11

A fictional ward has one monitor, one oxygen cage and two critical patients: a 5 kg cat with sepsis and a 40 kg dog with pneumonia. The technician must allocate the equipment for the next hour and justify the choice using measured variables rather than diagnosis.

Question 12

A fictional technician is asked to write the one-page standard for the practice's oxygen, blood pressure and feeding-tube checks. Write the four rules you would insist on, each with the error it prevents.
