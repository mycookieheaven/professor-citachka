Question 1

A fictional 9 kg French bulldog, four days after airway surgery, rests in an oxygen cage set to deliver roughly 50 percent oxygen. The technician notes the SpO2 has fallen from 96 to 91 percent, the respiratory rate has risen from 28 to 46 breaths per minute, and the patient is increasingly reluctant to lie down. The cage door has been opened repeatedly for treatments. Which reading of the situation is most defensible?

Question 2

A fictional 6 kg cat with a pyothorax is quiet and cold three days after a bite wound was cleaned. Temperature is 36.2 degrees Celsius, heart rate 118 beats per minute, respiratory rate 48 with open-mouth breathing, systolic pressure 104 mm Hg, lactate 3.9 mmol/L and urine output 0.4 mL/kg/h over two hours. A colleague says the cat cannot be septic because it is not febrile or tachycardic. Which response best applies the consensus definition?

Question 3

A fictional 11-year-old cat has a systolic reading of 192 mm Hg taken once, immediately after being lifted from a carrier, with a size 1 cuff on the forelimb while two people hold the cat in lateral recumbency. The veterinarian is about to start an antihypertensive. What should the technician say?

Question 4

A fictional 9-year-old dog with vomiting and weakness has a sodium of 129 mEq/L and a potassium of 6.9 mEq/L. The ECG shows a rate of 54 beats per minute with small P waves and a widened QRS. A colleague proposes correcting the sodium quickly to 140 mEq/L within four hours. What is the strongest objection?

Question 5

A fictional 4.6 kg cat in diabetic ketoacidosis is on an insulin infusion. At 14:00 the glucose is 402 mg/dL and at 15:00 it is 268 mg/dL. The technician is due to record the next value at 16:00. What is the best action at 15:00?
