Cumulative level test: Emergency Assessment, Stabilisation and Laboratory Reliability

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Saving a lesson records reading only. Previous lesson completions have not been converted into passing scores. You can continue reading without a pass; the assessment remains unpassed.

Question 1

Four fictional patients arrive within six minutes. Patient one is a dog with a gaping limb wound that continues to ooze through a dressing despite ten minutes of firm direct pressure. Patient two is a cat with a swollen painful abdomen and non-productive retching. Patient three is a dog with a chronic limp from a previous injury, eating and bright. Patient four is a rabbit with an audible inspiratory noise and laboured breathing. No veterinarian is free yet. Which plan best applies the order of assessment taught in this level?

Question 2

A fictional practice records that a patient deteriorated over 40 minutes and that each change was written in the record but never spoken to the veterinarian, because the team assumed that the record would be read. Which design change addresses the root of this problem most effectively?

Question 3

A fictional dog has an audible noise on inspiration with an extended neck and elbows out. A fictional cat in the next kennel has no audible noise but a respiratory rate of 48 per minute, exaggerated abdominal effort and loud sounds throughout the thorax on auscultation. A fictional guinea pig has a quiet chest, a very high respiratory rate and rapid shallow excursions. Which set of descriptions and actions best reflects the emergency guidance?

Question 4

A fictional cat has pink rather than red mucous membranes, a capillary refill time of two seconds, cold extremities and a measured blood pressure within the reference interval. Two days later the same cat, now deteriorating, has a heart rate below the lower reference value. Which reasoning is correct?

Question 5

A fictional patient has been in arrest for a few minutes. Using the guideline rate range of 100 to 120 compressions per minute, and a cycle defined as two minutes of uninterrupted compressions, a team pauses for 15 seconds in each minute to check a pulse. Which statement best describes the consequence?

Question 6

A fictional practice experiences an arrest in a patient recovering from a dental procedure. The crash cart is unlocked, the team has never rehearsed roles, no one records times, and afterwards nobody discusses the event. Which change would most improve the next event, according to the resuscitation literature?

Question 7

A fictional dog is presented 20 minutes after a bite wound to the lateral thorax. The surface wound is 1 centimetre across, there is surrounding swelling, and the patient is bright with pink membranes and a capillary refill time of one second. The owner asks whether the wound should be flushed with the antiseptic scrub they have at home and then closed with skin glue. Which response follows the emergency guidance?

Question 8

A fictional dog has a tibial fracture and a soft padded bandage applied while awaiting surgery. Six hours later the digits are cold and swollen, the patient is licking at the bandage, and the bandage has slipped. Which combination of principle and action is correct?

Question 9

A fictional household contains one dog and two cats. The dog is being treated with a topical antiparasitic that carries a label stating it is toxic to cats, and one cat has become twitchy and unsteady after grooming the dog. A second cat has no signs. The owner also asks whether the human painkiller in the cupboard could be used for the dog. Which set of statements is correct?

Question 10

A fictional eleven year old dog has heavy calculus, halitosis and gingival redness, with all teeth present on awake examination. The owner says the dog eats well, so the mouth must be fine, and asks for an annual awake scale and polish to avoid anaesthesia. Which response best reflects the guidelines and the literature?

Question 11

A fictional blood sample is collected at 08:05 from a struggling dog. It is a short draw placed into a large EDTA tube, mixed by shaking, wrapped loosely and placed directly against an ice pack, then centrifuged at 08:40 and analysed at 14:10. Results show a low haematocrit with a high mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration, a platelet count below the reference interval with clumps on the smear, and a raised potassium. Which explanation is most defensible?

Question 12

A fictional review examines two cases. In case one, a patient deteriorated and did not survive, and the record is incomplete, with several verbal exchanges undocumented. In case two, a patient recovered, but the record shows that an abnormal perfusion finding was never escalated and the team simply got lucky. Which conclusions follow from the discipline of reconstruction?
