Cumulative level test: Hospitalised Patient Nursing: Reading Depth, Comfort and Continuity

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Saving a lesson records reading only. Previous lesson completions have not been converted into passing scores. You can continue reading without a pass; the assessment remains unpassed.

Question 1

A fictional anaesthetist has left the theatre and a technician is the only trained person present. The dog on the table shows a rising heart rate and respiratory rate during clipping, the oximeter reading falls when the patient is repositioned, and the capnography waveform becomes distorted while the end-tidal value reads near normal. Rank and justify the correct sequence of actions.

Question 2

A fictional practice scores every patient with one generic pain scale and treats any score below its midpoint as comfortable. A cat recovering from abdominal surgery scores below the midpoint and is left without reassessment; a dog with osteoarthritis scores below the midpoint at a routine visit and the owner is reassured that nothing is needed. Analyse both uses.

Question 3

A fictional dog is forty minutes past extubation. It is lying laterally, has not swallowed, and its recorded temperature has fallen from the pre-anaesthetic value and continues to fall at each fifteen-minute check. The record for the last thirty minutes contains no other entries. A colleague suggests simply waiting, since the patient appears calm. Construct the defensible plan.

Question 4

A fictional patient is three days after removal of a skin mass. The technician finds the area around the incision larger than the recorded baseline, warm, and the patient resents approach. The technician's draft entry reads possible infection, consider antibiotics. Evaluate the entry and replace it.

Question 5

A fictional ward round discovers that a hospitalised dog has had no recorded urine output for a full shift, a urinary catheter collection system was left hanging at the level of the patient during a walk, and no intake figures exist for the day. Which statement best diagnoses the failure and describes the correct response?

Question 6

A fictional cat with reduced kidney function is on a long, slow fluid plan. Overnight the technician notes slightly increased respiratory effort, and the morning body weight is higher than the previous two mornings. The technician considers increasing the rate because the patient looks under-perfused, and also considers writing that the patient is about five per cent dehydrated. Evaluate both ideas.

Question 7

A fictional technician is asked to start a transfusion. The line in place contains a calcium-containing fluid, the only free pump is one normally used for intravenous fluids, the crossmatch on file was performed several weeks ago and no transfusion history is documented, and the patient has been vomiting. Which combination of concerns should be raised before anything is started?

Question 8

A fictional clinic wants to reduce hospital-acquired infection. It proposes buying additional personal protective equipment, adding a second hand-sanitiser dispenser in reception and running a refresher talk, while continuing to walk potentially infectious patients through the main waiting area and share stethoscopes between all rooms. Which plan best applies the published hierarchy?

Question 9

A fictional owner is anxious and telephones the hospital three times in an evening asking whether their dog will survive. The technician has the ward observations and knows the veterinarian plans to speak after the evening round. Construct the best response.

Question 10

A fictional hospitalised patient recovering from a laparotomy has four problems: an incision to protect, poor voluntary intake, intermittent pain, and a catheter in place. Write the structure of an integrated nursing plan and state what belongs to the technician and what does not.

Question 11

A fictional dog is recovering from emergency surgery performed at night and will remain on the ward for several days. The same technician is assigned to the patient from recovery through to discharge. Describe how the lessons of this level combine across that whole period, from depth and recovery judgement to the discharge conversation.

Question 12

Across this level, several tasks look clinical and several look clerical. A fictional student claims that only the drug decisions are clinically important and that the rest is documentation. Which summary best corrects that claim?
