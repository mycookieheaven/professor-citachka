Cumulative level test: Persons, reasons, and legitimate power: consciousness, responsibility, desert, and epistemic justice

Question 1

A tribunal reasons that a claimant cannot be a reliable witness to her own past because her self-account has changed over the years and she refuses to narrate the earlier period as her own. Diagnose the reasoning with the tools of this level.

Question 2

A student argues, first, that because no amount of physical detail entails experience, dualism is established, and second, that because a functionally identical duplicate without experience can be conceived, such a duplicate is possible. Assess both steps.

Question 3

An agent acts from her own settled reasons after deliberating as she normally does, and a device stands ready to compel her if she had wavered, so that she could not have acted otherwise. A colleague argues that blame is therefore misplaced. Evaluate.

Question 4

A legislator argues that people should bear the costs of their own bad choices and that unchosen disadvantages should be equalised. Which two objections from this level most directly threaten the position?

Question 5

Consider two flawed arguments. The first treats a threat of punishment as though it showed that the threatened person has an admirable character. The second attributes to an opponent a crude version of a view the opponent states with qualifications. Name the diagnosis for each.

Question 6

A state justifies a compulsory biometric registry by appeal to administrative efficiency in benefits and taxation. Residents in one district object that their records have repeatedly been wrong and that the objection procedure requires them to prove a negative. Assess the justification and the objection.

Question 7

Which claim about authority can a philosophical anarchist accept along with a defender of the service conception?

Question 8

A position dossier states a verdict at moderate confidence and lists one counter-argument. It includes no assessment of the author’s own reasoning and no statement of what would change her mind. What is missing, and why does it matter?

Question 9

Which pair of claims should a capstone defend about the tension among group-wise calibration, equal false positive rates, and equal false negative rates?

Question 10

An agent’s entire self-understanding, including the narrative she tells about her commitments, was installed by others before she could evaluate it. She now acts on it deliberately. How do the manipulation argument and the narrative view bear on this case?

Question 11

An agency accepts the output of an opaque predictive model as though it were testimony from a competent source, and denies any wrong on the ground that a program is not a person. Assess that reasoning.

Question 12

Once the arithmetic of base rates has fixed the menu of error distributions available to a policy, what remains to be decided, and by what argument?
