Cumulative level test: Meaning, modality, and the furniture of the world: reference, speech, necessity, time, and conceptual revision

Check understanding, separately from reading

Question 1

Two students dispute whether 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' is trivial. One says it must be, since it says of one object that it is itself; the other says it was a real astronomical discovery. Assess both claims.

Question 2

At a party a speaker says 'the man in the corner drinking a martini is a famous logician', indicating a man who is in fact drinking water. The man is a famous logician. A listener objects that the sentence is false because the description misdescribes him. Assess the objection.

Question 3

A couple exchange vows before a friend who has no licence to solemnise marriages, believing him to be authorised. The friend declares them married. A later dispute turns on whether a marriage occurred. Using the vocabulary of speech acts, assess.

Question 4

A carpenter makes a table from a particular block of oak. A philosopher says the table could have been made from a different block, since we can imagine a workshop in which a different block is used. Assess the inference.

Question 5

A city dweller cannot tell an elm from a beech and has never seen either closely, yet uses both words correctly and asks a botanist when precision matters. A theorist concludes that she does not really mean anything by 'elm'. Assess.

Question 6

'Eight is necessarily greater than seven. The number of planets is eight. Therefore the number of planets is necessarily greater than seven.' A student says the conclusion is false, since there might have been five planets, and concludes the argument is invalid. Assess.

Question 7

A barometer reading falls before every storm. A forecaster says the falling reading causes storms, and defends the claim by noting a perfect correlation, temporal priority, and spatial contiguity. Assess the claim.

Question 8

Two observers moving relative to one another disagree about whether two distant flashes are simultaneous. A presentist concludes that his view is untouched, since simultaneity is only a matter of description. Assess that reply.

Question 9

An argument runs: composition cannot be vague, so for any objects either they always compose something or they never do; we exist and are composed of particles; therefore any particles whatsoever compose something. A critic replies that noses and door-knobs are absurd consequences. Assess the exchange.

Question 10

A commission is asked whether patients with irreversible loss of all brain function are dead. One member says the question is empirical and neurology will answer it; another says it is purely a matter of word use and any answer will do. Assess both.

Question 11

'If you publish that story, your funding will be reviewed.' The editor calls it a prediction; the journalist calls it a threat. What does the dispute turn on, and how should it be resolved?

Question 12

A philosopher argues that because 'woman' is a social kind whose boundaries were fixed by contingent history, the term should be redefined to track a structural position, and that this redefinition is correct. Assess the step from the historical premise to the normative conclusion.
