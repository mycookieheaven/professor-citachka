Unit quiz: Meaning, reference, and what speakers do with words

Question 1

A newspaper profile states that Lois believes Superman can fly, and the same issue reports that Superman is Clark Kent. A subeditor concludes that Lois believes Clark Kent can fly, and is surprised when the sentence reads as false. Using Frege's distinction, what has gone wrong, and what does it show about the names inside the belief report?

Question 2

A minute records: 'The chair of the safety committee approved the override.' The committee had been dissolved a month earlier and had no chair. A member argues that because the description has no referent, both the minute's sentence and its negation are neither true nor false, so no finding of fault can be recorded. Is that reasoning sound?

Question 3

A reference letter reads in full: 'The candidate's command of medieval Latin is excellent, and his attendance was regular.' The hiring panel concludes that his philosophical work is weak. The candidate protests that the letter contains no such statement. Who is right about what the letter says, and on what does the panel's inference rest?

Question 4

A historian objects to the thesis that names are abbreviated descriptions: 'If Aristotle meant the teacher of Alexander, then the statement that Aristotle might not have taught Alexander would be false. But it is true, since he might have devoted himself to zoology. So the description is not the meaning of the name.' Reconstruct the argument and state what it establishes.

Question 5

Oscar on Earth and Twin Oscar on Twin Earth are molecule-for-molecule identical. Each says 'water is wet' of the clear liquid around him, which is H2O on Earth and XYZ on Twin Earth. A philosopher concludes that because their inner states are identical their words mean the same, and the difference lies only in their surroundings. Assess that conclusion.
