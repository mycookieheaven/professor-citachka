Cumulative level test: Validity, evidence, and fair evaluation: soundness, the fallacies that persuade, and the limits of what an argument can establish

Check understanding, separately from reading

12 questions · 80% to pass · no timer. This longer test revisits both units in the level. These authored questions assess recognition and application of the taught distinctions, not professional qualification. You may review the lessons and retry. Repeat attempts reuse the question bank; a remembered answer is not proof of transfer to a new situation.

Question 1

A coroner's report states that if the alarm had sounded, the staff would have evacuated, and that the staff did not evacuate, so the alarm did not sound. An inquiry adds that the alarm was later found to be functional and that the staff had been told to ignore it. Assess the argument's validity and soundness, and state what the additional facts do to each.

Question 2

A commentator responds to a study finding no effect of a school reform by observing that the reform's advocates have been funded by an interested party. A second commentator responds that the study is worthless because its author has a record of political activism. Compare the two responses.

Question 3

A philosopher proposes a description of a hypothetical case in which an agent knows a fact yet cannot act on it, and argues that this refutes a proposed definition of knowledge. A critic replies that the case is physically impossible and that the agent described could not exist. Evaluate the critic's reply.

Question 4

A researcher reports that a brain-imaging pattern in unresponsive patients predicts later recovery, and argues on that basis that these patients are conscious. Identify what the argument would need in order to license the stronger conclusion.

Question 5

A government advises citizens to accept a new public-health measure and to stop reading contrary analyses, saying that the evidence is settled and that public doubt is itself harmful. Assess this using the level's material on testimony and on the ethics of belief.

Question 6

A rule permits detention where an officer has a reasonable belief that a person poses a serious risk. Two officers apply it to the same facts and reach different conclusions, and a court asks whether the rule is void for vagueness. Supply the analysis the level's material supports.

Question 7

A study finds that regions with more doctors per capita have higher mortality rates, and a commentator concludes that doctors cause deaths. A second commentator replies that sicker populations attract more doctors. What is the structure of the second reply, and what further step would a policy argument need?

Question 8

A model predicts which defendants will reoffend accurately, and a court proposes to require judges to follow its recommendations. A critic argues that because the model's strongest predictors are prior convictions and neighbourhood, which are consequences of past enforcement, an intervention on them would not reduce reoffending. Assess the critic's argument.

Question 9

A columnist describes a proposal to lengthen prison sentences as the claim that punishment should be unlimited, refutes that claim by showing it would be barbaric, and declares the proposal defeated. A reader objects both to the description and to the inference. State both objections and the standard that would settle the matter.

Question 10

Two departments dispute whether a shared service saves money. Department A cites an aggregate comparison showing lower costs after the service began; Department B cites the same data stratified by case type showing higher costs in every stratum. Both are correct. Diagnose the dispute and state what would resolve it.

Question 11

A patient is told by a clinician that a treatment is appropriate, has read a study suggesting otherwise, and notes that the study was funded by a competitor. Using the level's material, identify the defeater and state what the patient is entitled to conclude.

Question 12

A researcher claims to understand why a treatment works because a model predicts its effect reliably, and a critic replies that without a mechanism the finding cannot be used to design a better treatment. Assess both claims using the level's material on explanation and on causation.
